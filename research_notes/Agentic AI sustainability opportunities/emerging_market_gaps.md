# Emerging-Market Sustainability Problems Underserved by Software (Africa, India, SE Asia, LatAm, Turkey) — 2024–2026

Scope note: Research done October 2026 with ~19 search/fetch calls. Many sources are trade press, law-firm summaries, or vendor pages; flagged where relevant. Items not verified in this session are placed in Gaps rather than stated as fact. Notation: "Inference" = my reasoning, not a sourced claim.

## 1. Exporter compliance burden (EUDR, textiles/garments, horticulture, buyer Scope 3 requests)

### Takeaway
EUDR is the most concrete, dated, payer-backed export-compliance problem in Africa: it applies from 30 Dec 2026 (large/medium operators) and 30 Jun 2027 (micro/small). The Commission's May 2026 review confirmed it will not be reopened, and that removes the "maybe it goes away" risk. The national traceability-system space is already filling with government-anointed vendors (Uganda, Ethiopia, Côte d'Ivoire). The open gap is mid-chain: exporters and cooperatives need due-diligence-statement (DDS) packaging, ERP/batch segregation and buyer data exchange. Textile (ESPR/DPP, EPR) and flower requirements are real but mostly come from buyers and are still in flux. Scope 3 questionnaire fatigue is well documented in general, but I found no emerging-market-specific quantification.

### Cited Findings
**EUDR timeline and scope (2026 status)**
- The EU formally adopted a further one-year EUDR delay on 19 Dec 2025. It now applies to medium/large operators from 30 Dec 2026, and most micro/small operators get an additional six months — [The Grocer](https://thegrocer.co.uk/sustainability-and-environment/african-countries-step-up-efforts-in-the-lead-up-to-eudr-amid-pushback/693732.article); [CropGPT summary](https://cropgpt.ai/west-africa-cocoa-eu-deforestation-regulation-implementation-delay)
- Commission simplification review published 4 May 2026. It does not reopen the EUDR, keeps the 30 Dec 2026 / 30 Jun 2027 dates, adds no "no-risk" country category, and postpones the country benchmarking review, so the May 2025 list remains the reference — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/eu-commission-publishes-simplification-review-of-eudr); [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102mrv4/eu-eudr-simplification-report-lands-alongside-updated-guidance-and-faqs); [OFI Magazine](https://www.ofimagazine.com/news/eu-commission-further-simplifies-eudr)
- The Commission estimates that measures since 2023 cut annual compliance costs for in-scope companies from about €8.1bn to about €2bn — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/eu-commission-publishes-simplification-review-of-eudr); [Jones Day](https://trendingnowinesg.jonesday.com/post/102mrow/the-eu-commission-publishes-its-report-on-the-simplification-of-the-eu-deforestat)
- Operators sourcing from low-risk countries still need a due-diligence system and must meet information requirements, but are spared risk assessment and mitigation under conditions — [EU-Japan Centre](https://www.eu-japan.eu/sites/default/files/imce/investineuhub/EUDR%20article/EUDR%20Article%E3%80%80%20(EN).pdf)
- A draft delegated act (feedback until 1 Jun 2026) proposes narrowing Annex I product scope. Leather is left open as a "political decision" — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/eu-commission-publishes-simplification-review-of-eudr)

**Who suffers (cocoa/coffee)**
- Traceability is "particularly acute" in Nigeria, Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana because supply chains are complex, farms are dispersed and digital infrastructure is limited. There is no comprehensive digital ID or farm mapping. Côte d'Ivoire is accelerating its national cocoa traceability system — [Africa Sustainability Matters, Aug 2026](https://africasustainabilitymatters.com/west-africas-cocoa-trade-faces-a-new-test-as-eu-deforestation-rules-raise-compliance-costs/); [The Grocer](https://thegrocer.co.uk/sustainability-and-environment/african-countries-step-up-efforts-in-the-lead-up-to-eudr-amid-pushback/693732.article)
- 91% of Ghanaian cocoa households are reported below the living-income benchmark, which limits their ability to absorb costs. Under Ghana's Concessions Act, naturally occurring trees on private land are vested in the state, which complicates tenure and trees-on-farm evidence — [The Grocer](https://thegrocer.co.uk/sustainability-and-environment/african-countries-step-up-efforts-in-the-lead-up-to-eudr-amid-pushback/693732.article)
- Fern (NGO) notes that geolocation needs a smartphone and internet. Cooperatives must manage data and keep batches physically separated, which "can be expensive". Fern argues the Ivorian system design puts costs on companies rather than farmers — [Julia Christian/Fern slides, UvA ACELG 2024](https://acelg.uva.nl/binaries/content/assets/subsites/amsterdam-centre-for-european-law-and-governance/events/2024/julia-christian-slides_eudr12-read-only.pdf)
- A Fairtrade-commissioned study (IDEF and BF Consult; commissioned by Commerce Équitable France, RICE, FTAO and AVSF; April 2024) costed ARS-1000 plus EUDR compliance for Ivorian cocoa cooperatives. The figures are in a PDF I could not access — [Fair Trade Advocacy Office](https://fairtrade-advocacy.org/posts/9-compliance-costs-of-cocoa-cooperatives-in-the-ivory-coast-with-the-eu-deforestation-regulation)
- A vendor-style page claims industry-wide cocoa EUDR costs of $1–3bn. No primary source was traceable, so treat it as unverified — [CPCON Group](https://cpcongroup.com/how-eudr-impacts-the-cocoa-supply-chain/)
- Uganda exported 8.4m 60-kg bags of coffee worth about US$2.4bn (Nov 2024–Oct 2025), as cited by Dimitra — [Dimitra/Medium](https://dimitratech.medium.com/uganda-approves-dimitra-as-official-traceability-provider-e2be12ed2f62)

**Existing tools (EUDR)**
- Uganda approved Dimitra as one of five official national coffee traceability/EUDR due-diligence providers. Its Plutus Coffee partnership targets about 90,000 t/yr (company claim) — [Dimitra/Medium](https://dimitratech.medium.com/uganda-approves-dimitra-as-official-traceability-provider-e2be12ed2f62)
- Ethiopia: local firm Vulcan ICT contracted by the Ethiopian Coffee & Tea Authority (ECTA) to build a national coffee traceability platform. The EU and Norway's Development Fund convened stakeholders in Jan 2026 on cadastre and digital tracking. Land registration is being rolled out in coffee woredas — [Shega](https://shega.co/news/ethiopia-races-to-meet-imminent-eu-deforestation-regulation-with-coffee-traceability-platform); [CropGPT](https://cropgpt.ai/ethiopia-coffee-eudr-compliance-and-digital-traceability-integration)
- TraceX markets EUDR platforms for Ugandan and Ethiopian exporters. It cites paper-based systems, cooperative-level data gaps and limited farm mapping as the pain points (vendor claims) — [TraceX Uganda](https://tracextech.com/?p=22655); [TraceX Ethiopia](https://tracextech.com/eudr-dds/coffee-supply-chain-ethiopia/)

**Textiles/garments (North Africa)**
- EU imported €2.5bn of Tunisian textiles in 2025 (19% of EU imports from Tunisia). The EU took 73% of Tunisian exports. The EU-funded UNEP InTex phase 2 (Jun 2026–Apr 2028) supports five Tunisian SMEs, i.e. donor-driven capacity building — [Managers.tn, Sep 2026](https://managers.tn/2026/09/09/textile-tunisien-5-pme-se-preparent-aux-nouvelles-exigences-europeennes/)
- EU co-legislators provisionally agreed mandatory textile EPR in Feb 2025, with eco-modulated fees. Textile DPP content depends on a delegated act still being finalised. Sources disagree on how firm the "2027" DPP date is — [Première Vision](https://www.premierevision.com/articles/15067446-2c50-f011-8f7c-000d3a2e1c0a/insights-on-the-evolving-regulatory-landscape-in-textiles-and-fashion-); [TraceX DPP](https://tracextech.com/digital-product-passports-for-textiles/)
- Buyers are expected to use DPP data as a supplier qualification requirement. Self-declared carbon and recycled-content claims "no longer sufficient" (vendor analysis) — [TraceX DPP](https://tracextech.com/digital-product-passports-for-textiles/); [Mesopartner](https://www.mesopartner.com/post/digital-product-passport-a-game-changer-for-textile-compliance)

**Horticulture (Kenya flowers)**
- Kenyan growers launched a Carbon Reduction and Opportunities Toolkit measuring water, energy and carbon from farm to market, in response to EU market fears (undated article) — [HortiDaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/6003641/kenya-flower-farmers-launch-kit-to-counter-eu-market-fears/)
- Flower export value figures conflict. The Star cites AFA at Sh81.3bn in 2025 and horticulture at Sh143.78bn, while IFTEX organisers cite about KES 110bn — [The Star, Jun 2026](https://www.the-star.co.ke/counties/central/2026-06-02-flower-exports-bloom-to-over-sh143bn-amid-rising-demand); [AgriFocus Africa](https://agrifocusafrica.com/2026/06/02/iftex-2026-opens-in-nairobi-as-industry-leaders-call-for-sustainability-market-expansion-and-stronger-trade-partnerships/)

**Buyer Scope 3 questionnaires**
- CSRD-obligated companies push Scope 3 and ESG data requests onto SME suppliers. A statutory cap on what can be requested applies from 2027 — [BDO Poland alert](https://www.bdo.pl/getattachment/33325595-043d-4733-8cfe-a32758798026/ALERT-ESG-1_08_26-EN.pdf)
- Under Omnibus, SMEs with <1,000 employees can refuse requests beyond the VSME standard. A critique of the May 2026 draft argues Scope 3 sits outside the cap, which "guarantees bespoke questionnaires" — [Fiegenbaum Solutions](https://www.fiegenbaum.solutions/en/blog/eu-value-chain-cap-five-gaps-voluntary-standard)
- Suppliers get emission requests in incompatible templates and timeframes, which leads to slow, incomplete or non-responses — [DitchCarbon](https://ditchcarbon.com/blog/reducing-supplier-fatigue-race-to-net-zero)
- Malaysian listed companies must disclose value-chain (Scope 3) emissions, which pushes requests onto Malaysian and regional suppliers — [Elite Asia](https://www.eliteasia.co/bursa-malaysia-esg-updates/)

### Inferences
- Government-mandated national traceability systems (Uganda's five approved providers, Ethiopia's ECTA/Vulcan, Côte d'Ivoire's national system) mean farm-level mapping is becoming a regulated, procured layer. That is a hard wedge for a small startup. The better gap is the exporter/cooperative middle layer: reconciling national-system plot IDs with ERP batches, mass-balance and segregation, DDS generation for EU operators, and answering importer queries. This is close to carbonTraceAI's ERP-integration skill set.
- EUDR's payer is clear: EU operators and traders such as chocolate makers and coffee roasters, plus exporters who risk losing access. That is commercial demand. Smallholder-level work is mostly donor/government funded.
- Textiles in Morocco, Egypt and Tunisia are a 2027–2028 opportunity (DPP/EPR), not a 2026 one. Today's demand is buyer-driven and donor-subsidised (InTex).
- A "one data room, many questionnaires" agent for exporters could be commercial. It would map one verified dataset (VSME-style plus CBAM/EUDR plus PCF) onto many buyer templates such as CDP, EcoVadis and bespoke Excel. The pain is documented in the EU, but I found no emerging-market quantification.

### Gaps
- No primary per-cooperative or per-tonne EUDR cost figure was obtainable (the Fairtrade/IDEF PDF was not accessible).
- Cameroon and the Ghana COCOBOD traceability system status were not researched. Rubber and timber EUDR specifics were not covered.
- I found no Morocco- or Egypt-specific textile buyer-requirement data. Ethiopian and Kenyan garments (EPZ/AGOA dynamics) were not covered.
- No Kenya Flower Council certification or carbon-label requirements were found.
- No ITC SME Competitiveness Outlook data on questionnaire burden for African or Indian SMEs was found.

## 2. Carbon markets (Article 6, national registries, MRV, integrity)

### Takeaway
Article 6 is now operational in Africa. Ghana has issued and transferred ITMOs and authorised 12.7 MtCO2e through 2030. Kenya has regulations, a DNA (NEMA) and an announced 10m ITMO cap for 2026–2030. This creates sovereign-side work: authorisation, corresponding adjustments, registry and reporting. It also creates developer-side work: authorisation applications, benefit-sharing and MRV documentation. Integrity scandals (Kariba, cookstove over-crediting) raise the value of credible digital MRV. Buyers are DFIs, governments and developers, but voluntary carbon market (VCM) revenues are volatile.

### Cited Findings
- Kenya: the Climate Change (Carbon Markets) Regulations 2024 were gazetted 17 May 2024. They set out approval and monitoring procedures aligned with Article 6 and prohibit double counting. NEMA has been DNA since 12 Jun 2024 and issues letters of approval and maintains the registry — [Mzizi Africa datahub](https://datahub-mzizi-africa.notion.site/Development-and-Regulation-of-Carbon-Projects-in-Kenya-1e7a46d8343c803e818dce04ead6b170); [FSD Kenya/Article Six Group, Jun 2026](https://www.fsdkenya.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Unlocking-Kenyas-Carbon-Market-Potential-FSD-Kenya-Article-Six-Group-June-2026.pdf)
- Kenya will limit Article 6 transfers to 10m ITMOs over 2026–2030. Analysts say this may push developers back to the VCM — [Carbon Pulse, Jun 2026](https://carbon-pulse.com/522403/)
- Kenya has Article 6.2 agreements with Switzerland and Sweden, and Singapore talks are advanced. Sources conflict on the registry: FSD says a national carbon registry launched in Feb 2026, while SDSG (Apr 2026) says it is still being finalised — [FSD Kenya](https://www.fsdkenya.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Unlocking-Kenyas-Carbon-Market-Potential-FSD-Kenya-Article-Six-Group-June-2026.pdf); contradicted by [SDSG](https://www.sdsg.org/blog-five/2026/4/30/examining-article-6-readiness)
- Kenya received about €4m (≈KSh590m) EU support for MRV, and on 5 Oct 2026 launched a guide on carbon-market engagement — [Africa Sustainability Matters](https://africasustainabilitymatters.com/?p=27610)
- Ghana has authorised 12.7 MtCO2e of ITMOs through 2030, more than 50% of its Article 6 carbon budget, "as project pipeline surges" (Sep 2026) — [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/546612/)
- Ghana issued its first Article 6 units, transferring nearly 12,000 ITMOs to Switzerland (Jul 2025). It operationalised digital ITMO trading and settlement with Singapore (Nov 2024) — [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/415154/); [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/342007/?site=cpp)
- A Swiss NGO alleged that a Ghana–Switzerland Article 6.2 cookstove project over-credits (allegation, Nov 2024) — [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/345385/)
- Malawi filed its initial Article 6 report to the UN, which shows more countries entering the pipeline — [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/426119/)
- Kariba REDD+ (Zimbabwe) faced over-crediting scrutiny from 2023, and its contract was terminated. South Pole disputes over-issuance. Researchers say Verra's remediation plan exposes "deep structural flaws" — [Transparency International](https://www.transparency.org/projects/climate-governance-integrity-programme/climate-corruption-atlas/kariba-redd-project-faces-scrutiny-after-carbon-credit-integrity-concerns-and-contract-termination); [South Pole](https://southpole.com/news/fact-check-kariba-redd-has-not-over-issued-nor-will-ever-over-issue-carbon-credits-here-is-why); [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/447644/)
- Gill-Wiehl, Kammen and Haya (Nature Sustainability, 2024) found pervasive over-crediting across the five cookstove methodologies behind almost all cookstove credits. The sample was over-credited about 9.2×, with Gold Standard's metered methodology closest at 1.5× — [Berkeley Carbon Trading Project](https://dev.gspp.berkeley.edu/berkeley-carbon-trading-project/cookstoves); [Notre Dame-hosted manuscript](https://strand.nd.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2026-08/Pervasive_overcrediting_maintext.pdf)
- SDSG describes African Article 6 readiness as "a balancing act without guardrails". Institutional capacity lags deal-making — [SDSG](https://www.sdsg.org/blog-five/2026/4/30/examining-article-6-readiness)

### Inferences
- Software demand splits two ways. (a) Host-government Article 6 ops: authorisation workflows, corresponding-adjustment accounting, reporting to the UNFCCC. These are tender/donor-funded (EU's €4m to Kenya) and often go to UNDP, World Bank or consultancies. (b) Developer-side documentation, data-room and MRV work: commercial, but tied to credit prices. Option (b) is the closer fit for a small agentic team: drafting authorisation applications, PDD/monitoring reports, benefit-sharing calculations, evidence packs for buyers and ratings agencies.
- Kenya's ITMO cap and Ghana's >50% budget authorisation mean scarce sovereign authorisation becomes a competitive filing process. Application quality and speed matter, which is a plausible agent use case.
- Metered and digital MRV (the Gold Standard metered methodology was the most accurate) is where integrity premiums sit. This favours IoT/data pipelines over pure document generation.

### Gaps
- Zambia and Rwanda Article 6 deals were not found in this session. ACMI targets and progress were not found (the search returned nothing on ACMI). Nigeria NCMAF status was not researched.
- No data on developer MRV/documentation costs per project (consultant fees, VVB costs) for Africa.

## 3. Climate finance access (GCF/GEF/AF proposals, DFI ESG DD, green bonds, SME SLLs)

### Takeaway
The finance gap is enormous and documented: Africa received about $44bn/yr (2021/22), which covers only 23% of NDC needs. Access friction (accreditation, proposal preparation, pipeline bankability) is widely cited as the bottleneck. Recent quantification of proposal preparation cost and time was not found. The ultimate funders are multilaterals, while proposal preparers are national entities and consultants. This is partly donor economics, but DFIs and banks needing ESG DD at scale is a commercial angle.

### Cited Findings
- CPI (commissioned by FSD Africa): Africa climate finance rose ~48% from ~$29.5bn (2019/20) to ~$43.7bn (2021/22). Only 23% of estimated NDC implementation needs are met, and flows must at least quadruple annually to 2030. Mitigation needs are 18% met and adaptation 20% — [CPI Landscape of Climate Finance in Africa 2024](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/publication/landscape-of-climate-finance-in-africa-2024/); [CPI press release](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/press-release/climate-finance-in-africa-2024-press-release/)
- International sources supply 87% of the total and domestic actors ~10% ($4.2bn), mostly private. Government funding fell from $1.6bn to $1.0bn. The top 10 countries receive 50% and the bottom 30 receive 10% — [CPI press release](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/press-release/climate-finance-in-africa-2024-press-release/); [African Review](https://africanreview.com/finance/climate-finance-to-africa-falling-short)
- GCA official: African countries struggle to access GCF money because of "complicated accreditation procedures". Ghana runs accreditation and proposal development in parallel — [Ghana News Agency](https://gna.org.gh/?p=213019)
- A Kenyan consultancy argues most GCF failures occur at the readiness stage. Readiness support can reach up to $7m per country (consultant claim) — [ACAL Consulting](https://acalconsulting.co.ke/our-insights/gcf-access-africa-institutional-readiness)
- Example: Rwanda GCF readiness request (needs assessment and NDA capacity), 18 months, ~$297,677 — [GCF](https://www.greenclimate.fund/document/strengthening-rwandas-capacity-access-gcf-funds-through-assessment-readiness-needs-updating)

### Inferences
- Payers for proposal-drafting agents would be accredited entities (national DFIs, ministries, NGOs) using readiness grants, or consultancies wanting productivity. The market is real but grant-funded and procurement-heavy, so a small startup would need to sell to consultancies or DFIs rather than governments.
- The more commercial angle is DFI/bank-side ESG due diligence: IFC Performance Standards screening of SME borrowers, green-loan eligibility tagging, sustainability-linked-loan KPI monitoring. This is not directly evidenced here and needs validation.

### Gaps
- No recent quantitative data on GCF/GEF/AF proposal preparation time or cost. No data on IFC PS DD costs, African green bond issuance (Kenya, Nigeria, Egypt, SA) or SME sustainability-linked loan uptake. These were not researched within budget.

## 4. Energy (South Africa wheeling/PPAs, mini-grids, JETPs)

### Takeaway
South Africa's private wheeling market is scaling: multi-buyer wind and solar PPAs such as the Impofu cluster for Sasol/Air Liquide and Lion Thorn for PPC, plus virtual wheeling and a planned wholesale market (SAWEM). This creates real, commercial complexity in matching, settlement, billing reconciliation and Scope 2/REC evidence for C&I buyers. JETP money flows slowly and mostly as loans. It is largely a sovereign/DFI arena.

### Cited Findings
- The 2021 Electricity Regulation Act amendment removed licensing below a threshold and allowed wheeling to multiple customers — [Duo Marketing](https://www.duomarketing.co.za/?p=23553); [Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr on virtual wheeling](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2023/Sectors/Energy/energy-and-power-alert-13-September-virtual-wheeling-agreements-a-new-frontier-in-renewable-energy-supply-solutions)
- Impofu North (112.1 MW, Enel Green Power) began commercial operation in May 2026. It wheels to Sasol and Air Liquide over a 116 km, 132 kV private line, the largest privately built powerline in SA — [Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/enel-green-power-rsa-brings-online-330-mw-impofu-wind-farm-cluster-underpinned-by-three-long-term-power-purchase-agreements-ppas-with-sasol-and-air-liquide-a-landmark-renewable-energy-project-for-south-africas-private-sector-2026-07-02/rep_id:5872/company:sawea-2026-04-07); [ChemXplore](https://chemxplore.com/news/enel-green-power-impofu-wind-cluster)
- Lion Thorn solar (49 MWp, POWERX) reached financial close, with 20-year wheeled PPAs including PPC (cement) and COD targeted for 2028 — [Africa Sustainability Matters](https://africasustainabilitymatters.com/south-africas-49-mwp-lion-thorn-solar-park-reaches-financial-close-as-private-renewable-power-investment-accelerates/)
- Virtual wheeling lets IPPs serve customers in municipalities "in good financial standing" with Eskom, which opens smaller C&I clients. SAWEM was targeted for 2026, and its launch was not confirmed as of Oct 2026 — [Duo Marketing](https://www.duomarketing.co.za/?p=23553); [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/print-version/wholesale-market-bodes-well-for-energy-stability-2026-01-23)
- Energize discusses post-signing operational issues with wheeling deals — [Energize](https://www.energize.co.za/article/beyond-the-ppa-what-really-happens-after-wheeling-deals-are-signed?page=108)
- JETP pledges: South Africa $8.5bn (2021), Indonesia $20bn, Vietnam $15.5bn (2022). The 2024 ISEAS analysis found a 70% financing gap for Indonesia and 89% for Vietnam. IPG had disbursed $1.2bn cash to Indonesia as of Jul 2025, with $5–6bn more in the pipeline — [ISEAS 2024/40](https://www.iseas.edu.sg/articles-commentaries/iseas-perspective/2024-40-just-energy-transition-partnerships-jetps-in-indonesia-and-vietnam-implications-for-southeast-asia-by-melinda-martinus/); [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/421400/)

### Inferences
- An SA C&I energy-ops agent is a commercially payer-backed niche, used by traders, IPPs and buyers. It would reconcile Eskom and municipal bills against wheeling credits, track PPA performance and produce Scope 2 market-based evidence. It also links to CBAM: indirect emissions and SA exporters' grid factors.
- JETPs are not a startup market directly. Value accrues to sovereigns, DFIs and large consultancies.

### Gaps
- Eskom wheeling tariffs, NERSA rules, the load-shedding status in 2025–26 and SAWEM launch status were not verified. Mini-grid operator ops (Nigeria DARES, Kenya KOSAP) and the Senegal JETP were not researched. Any US withdrawal from JETPs was not verified in this session.

## 5. Local regulation (South Africa carbon tax/reporting, ISSB adoption, Egypt, Morocco, Turkey)

### Takeaway
South Africa has Africa's most binding domestic carbon regime. The carbon tax rose to R236/t in 2025 with R308 signalled for 2026. The Climate Change Act (Jul 2024) adds carbon budgets with a R640/t penalty rate. This creates recurring compliance work for emitters, which are a clear payer. ISSB adoption in Nigeria (PIEs from 2028 per draft) and Kenya (proposed 2027) creates future listed-company demand. Turkey's ETS pilot (MRV-only in 2026) is a fresh, CBAM-linked market.

### Cited Findings
- SA carbon tax went from R190/tCO2e (2024) to R236 (from 1 Jan 2025), a 24% rise. R308 is signalled for 2026 in the Phase 2 path. Under the Climate Change Act, emissions above the approved carbon budget face R640/t. Treasury proposed replacing the 5% carbon-budget allowance with a larger offset allowance from 2026 — [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/za/news/updates-to-the-south-african-carbon-tax-2025/); [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/southafrica/climate-change/1545424/south-africa-looks-to-strengthen-carbon-tax-for-2026-new-measures-to-drive-emissions-reductions)
- The Climate Change Act was signed 23 Jul 2024. Proposed rules could see executives imprisoned for reporting failures (press report on proposals, not final law) — [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/za/news/updates-to-the-south-african-carbon-tax-2025/); [Daily Investor](https://dailyinvestor.com/south-africa/96754/south-africa-plans-jail-time-and-tax-hikes-under-new-emissions-rules/)
- Note: "NGER" is Australia's scheme. SA reporting sits under the National GHG Emission Reporting Regulations (air quality legislation). Thresholds were not verified in this session.
- Nigeria: FRC issued an amended ISSB roadmap and guideline on 23 Feb 2026, updating the 2024 roadmap. The Jan 2026 draft proposed PIEs from 2028 and SMEs from 2030, with assurance delayed one year. Final dates are unconfirmed — [FRC Nigeria](https://frcnigeria.gov.ng/2026/02/26/frc-unveils-amended-roadmap-and-sustainability-reporting-guideline-to-strengthen-adoption-of-ifrs-sustainability-disclosure-standards-in-nigeria/); [XBRL International](https://www.xbrl.org/news/nigeria-consults-on-its-revised-issb-roadmap/)
- Kenya: ICPAK proposed PIE adoption by 2027 (older source). Ghana, Zimbabwe and Egypt are "progressing" with no firm Ghana date found — [Grant Thornton SA](https://www.grantthornton.co.za/insights2/ifrs-s1--s2-a-new-era-for-sustainability-reporting-in-africa/); [BDO East Africa](https://bdo-ea.com/en-gb/insights/the-future-of-sustainability-reporting-in-africa-key-insights-on-issb-and-ifrs-sustainability-discl)
- Turkey: Climate Law No. 7552 took effect in Jul 2025. The TR ETS pilot covers electricity, cement, iron and steel, aluminium and fertilisers, with installations ≥50 ktCO2e mandatory. 2026 is MRV only, targets apply to 2027 emissions, and surrender is due by end-Nov 2028. A 5-year GHG permit is required and verified reports are due by end-April. The ETS Regulation was published 27 Aug 2026. Allocation is 100% free and offsets are barred in the pilot (law-firm summary) — [Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/what-does-the-turkish-emissions-trading-system-introduce); [Kesikli/Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/doing-business-in/the-emissions-trading-system-under-turkish-law/); [Carbon Market Watch](https://carbonmarketwatch.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Turkish-ETS_final-version.pdf)
- CBAM allows deduction for carbon price "effectively paid" in the origin country, so Turkey's ETS directly affects exporters' CBAM bills — [Mondaq Turkey](https://www.mondaq.com/turkey/economic-analysis/1771518/navigating-the-2026-carbon-frontier-preparing-turkish-industry-for-simplified-cbam-compliance)

### Inferences
- The highest-fit adjacency for carbonTraceAI is an install-level MRV and permit-and-report engine. It would serve SA carbon tax and GHG reporting, Turkey's ETS MRV (2026) and India's CCTS (section 6), all on the same calculation core as CBAM. One emissions dataset would feed domestic compliance plus CBAM "carbon price paid" evidence.
- ISSB in Nigeria and Kenya is 2027–2028 demand, concentrated in listed companies and banks served by Big 4. The startup angle is subsidiary or value-chain data collection rather than the report itself.

### Gaps
- Egypt FRA ESG rules, Morocco regulation and the JSE sustainability disclosure guidance were not researched. SA GHG reporting thresholds and the confirmed 2026 carbon tax rate were not verified.

## 6. India-specific (CCTS, BRSR Core, CBAM for MSMEs)

### Takeaway
India has the largest near-term compliance market in this scope. 490 CCTS obligated entities have GEI targets (FY2023-24 baseline), and the first compliance filing is around 31 Jul 2026. Iron and steel and fertiliser targets are pending. CBAM is hitting MSMEs hard: steel and aluminium exports to the EU fell 24.4% in FY25, and data, not technology, is the bottleneck. BRSR value-chain disclosure was softened to voluntary, which weakens that driver.

### Cited Findings
- CCTS was notified 28 Jun 2023 under the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022 and builds on PAT — [Oren](https://www.orennow.com/blog/carbon-credit-trading-scheme-ccts-india); [KPMG India](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2024/04/chapter-2-development-of-carbon-credit-and-green-credit.pdf)
- First GEI targets (Oct 2025) covered aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali and pulp and paper, with 282 entities. The second batch (13 Jan 2026) added refineries, petrochemicals, textiles and secondary aluminium, with 208 entities, for 490 in total. Iron and steel and fertiliser are pending. Compliance years are 2025-26 and 2026-27 against a 2023-24 baseline. Over-performers earn tradable CCCs on power exchanges, and under-performers must buy and surrender — [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/compliance-obligations-under-indias-carbon-credit-trading-scheme-enter-force-seven-sectors); [Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/govt-notifies-greenhouse-gas-emission-intensity-targets-for-208-more-carbon-intensive-industries-1503544850.html); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/india-sets-first-ever-ghg-emission-intensity-targets-under-ccts); [Renewable Watch](https://renewablewatch.in/2026/01/28/goi-notifies-greenhouse-gas-emission-intensity-targets-for-carbon-intensive-sectors/)
- ICAP: first compliance date 31 Jul for the 2025-26 year. The deadline wording differs across sources — [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/compliance-obligations-under-indias-carbon-credit-trading-scheme-enter-force-seven-sectors); [Oren](https://www.orennow.com/blog/carbon-credit-trading-scheme-ccts-india)
- BRSR Core: value-chain disclosures for the top 250 were originally comply-or-explain from FY24-25, with assurance from FY25-26. SEBI (Mar 2025) deferred them and made them voluntary. Value-chain assessment or assurance is voluntary from FY26-27, and a new "assessment" option allows non-auditor providers — [Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2025/04/brsr-disclosures-for-value-chain-partners-eased-by-sebi/); [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/brsr-all-you-need-to-know/); [SEBI circular Jul 2023](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/SEBI_Circular_12072023.pdf)
- CBAM in India: GTRI reports steel and aluminium exports to the EU fell 24.4%, from $7.71bn (FY24) to $5.82bn (FY25), with some firms exiting. Exporters may need 15–22% price cuts. MSMEs are hardest hit by data and verification costs, and large producers often don't pass plant-level emissions data downstream — [Business Today, Jun 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/industry/story/eus-new-carbon-tax-could-hit-thousands-of-indian-msmes-can-they-adapt-536301-2026-06-11); [Basis Point Insight](https://www.basispointinsight.com/Story/-india-s-metal-exports-to-eu-plunge-24--as-cbam-reporting-hits-msmes-_30e1360663c9.html)
- A fastener and steel MSME exporter pays a consultant about Rs 2 lakh/yr for CBAM, because it has no in-house capacity — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/how-the-eus-carbon-border-tax-is-reshaping-indian-msmes)
- FISME: SMEs depend on grid power (80–85% coal), while large firms use captive plants — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/how-the-eus-carbon-border-tax-is-reshaping-indian-msmes)
- Estimates of the trade impact conflict. ICRIER/Goldar et al. 2026 estimate about a 24% fall in EU steel exports, while Majumder et al. 2024 find it negligible — [Vajiram & Ravi summary](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/eus-cbam-begins-impact-on-indias-steel-and-aluminium-exports/)
- Takshashila: India has "not significantly advanced" CBAM preparedness. The EU says CBAM is non-negotiable in FTA talks — [Takshashila, Mar 2026](https://takshashila.org.in/content/blogs/20260316-India-s-CBAM-preparedness.html)
- The EU plans to extend CBAM to about 180 more steel and aluminium downstream products from Jan 2028 — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/industry/story/eus-new-carbon-tax-could-hit-thousands-of-indian-msmes-can-they-adapt-536301-2026-06-11)

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay for Indian MSME CBAM is anchored at about Rs 2 lakh/yr (≈$2.3k) of consultant fees. SaaS must be priced below that, which means very low ACV. Cluster or association channels (FISME, Ludhiana fasteners, EEPC) are needed.
- The 2028 downstream expansion to about 180 products will multiply the MSME population. Upstream data scarcity (mills not sharing plant-level data) suggests a supplier-data-exchange or default-value-optimisation agent.
- CCTS's 490 obligated entities are large companies (BEE/PAT veterans with existing consultants), but the MRV, verification and CCC-trading workflow is new. The same calc engine could serve CCTS GEI tracking plus CBAM.
- BRSR value-chain demand is weaker after voluntarisation. Do not build a business on it.

### Gaps
- No tool-landscape data on Indian CCTS/CBAM vendors was gathered. No count of Indian MSME CBAM exporters was found.

## 7. Clear payers vs donor-only economics (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Clear commercial payers exist where a binding regulation sits on a company with revenue at stake: EUDR exporters and EU operators, CBAM exporters (India, Turkey, Africa), SA carbon tax payers, India CCTS obligated entities, Turkey ETS installations, and SA wheeling and PPA counterparties. Donor and tender economics dominate smallholder traceability, host-government Article 6 infrastructure, GCF readiness and SME textile capacity building.

### Cited Findings
- Donor-funded examples: EU €4m to Kenya for carbon-market MRV — [Africa Sustainability Matters](https://africasustainabilitymatters.com/?p=27610); EU/UNEP InTex for Tunisian textile SMEs — [Managers.tn](https://managers.tn/2026/09/09/textile-tunisien-5-pme-se-preparent-aux-nouvelles-exigences-europeennes/); EU/Norway Development Fund support for Ethiopian coffee cadastre — [CropGPT](https://cropgpt.ai/ethiopia-coffee-eudr-compliance-and-digital-traceability-integration); GCF readiness grants — [GCF Rwanda](https://www.greenclimate.fund/document/strengthening-rwandas-capacity-access-gcf-funds-through-assessment-readiness-needs-updating)
- Government-procured traceability: Uganda's five approved providers — [Dimitra](https://dimitratech.medium.com/uganda-approves-dimitra-as-official-traceability-provider-e2be12ed2f62); Ethiopia ECTA–Vulcan ICT — [Shega](https://shega.co/news/ethiopia-races-to-meet-imminent-eu-deforestation-regulation-with-coffee-traceability-platform)
- Commercial, regulation-forced spend: SA carbon tax at R236/t — [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/za/news/updates-to-the-south-african-carbon-tax-2025/); CCTS CCC purchase obligations — [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/compliance-obligations-under-indias-carbon-credit-trading-scheme-enter-force-seven-sectors); Turkish ETS verified reporting — [Ember](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/what-does-the-turkish-emissions-trading-system-introduce); MSME CBAM consultant fees — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/how-the-eus-carbon-border-tax-is-reshaping-indian-msmes)

### Inferences: ranked candidate problem areas for carbonTraceAI (edge = ERP integration plus calc engine plus Africa focus)
1. **Multi-regime facility MRV engine.** One plant dataset produces CBAM reports plus SA carbon tax/GHG reporting, Turkey ETS MRV (2026), India CCTS GEI and "carbon price paid" evidence. Payers: emitters/exporters. Strongest fit, since it reuses the existing calc engine.
2. **EUDR exporter/cooperative middle layer.** Batch segregation in ERP, linking national-system plot IDs to shipments, DDS packs for EU operators, importer query handling. Payers: exporters and EU operators. Deadline 30 Dec 2026. Avoid competing with government-procured farm mapping.
3. **Supplier-questionnaire and data-room agent for EM exporters.** Answer CDP, EcoVadis, VSME, PCF and bespoke buyer templates from one verified dataset. Payers: exporters, or buyers subsidising supplier tooling. Demand is evidenced in general, unquantified for EM.
4. **Carbon project developer documentation/MRV copilot.** Article 6 authorisation filings (Kenya cap, Ghana budget), monitoring reports, integrity evidence. Payers: developers. Revenue is volatile, so integrity-grade metered data has the premium.
5. **SA C&I wheeling/PPA ops and Scope 2 evidence.** Payers: IPPs, traders, industrial buyers. Commercial but SA-specific.
6. **Climate-finance proposal and DFI ESG-DD agents.** Mostly donor or tender. Sell to consultancies or DFIs, not governments.

### Gaps
- No primary evidence on willingness to pay for EUDR SaaS among African exporters, and no pricing benchmarks for EM sustainability SaaS.
- LatAm (Brazil coffee, soy and cattle EUDR; Colombia; Chile) and Southeast Asia (Indonesia and Malaysia palm/rubber EUDR, Vietnam coffee) were not researched in this session. Both are large EUDR exposure regions with established local vendors.
