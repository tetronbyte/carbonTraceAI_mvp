# Agentic AI in Sustainability/Climate: Players, Capabilities, Funding (2024-2026)

Scope note: Research done October 2026. CBAM tools and carbon-accounting platforms (Watershed, Sweep, Persefoni, Greenly) were covered earlier in `reports/CBAM agentic workflow market research.md` and are only mentioned briefly here. Where possible, "shipped" (GA product, named customers, deployment numbers) is kept separate from "announced" (launch post, targets, marketing claims). Most capability claims below come from vendor press releases and have not been independently verified.

## Which startups (2024-2026) explicitly build AI agents for sustainability tasks, what do the agents do, and what are their funding, customers and pricing?

### Takeaway
"Agentic" sustainability products that clearly ship and have clear traction fall into four groups. (1) Power and project development agents for permitting, siting and interconnection: PermitFlow, Paces, GridCARE, ThinkLabs. (2) Building-operations agents: BrainBox AI/Trane, PassiveLogic, Facilio. (3) Regulation-driven supply-chain due diligence and trade platforms: Altana, Prewave, osapiens, Sourcemap. (4) Incumbent reporting suites adding agents: Workiva. Pure-play ESG reporting agent startups (Briink and similar) are small and consolidating. Carbon-credit MRV/rating companies are being acquired rather than raising money. No funded startup was found that focuses specifically on green-claims/greenwashing agents.

### Inferences
- The strongest "agent" products are workflow-bound and tied to a deadline or permit: permit applications, interconnection filings, customs entries, BMS set-points. Each has a clear end state, a clear owner and money lost to delay. Open-ended ESG "copilots" (Clarity AI, early Briink) are assistants more than agents.
- Strategic acquirers are absorbing the category: Trane/BrainBox, Carbon Direct/Pachama, Position Green/Greenomy, ISS STOXX/Sust Global, Altana/Cervo. For a small player, being bought by an incumbent that owns distribution looks like a more realistic exit than reaching scale alone.
- Incumbents like Workiva now ship disclosure agents in the CSRD/ISSB workflow. Pure-play CSRD agent startups have little room unless they own a niche (for example SME/VSME, a specific supply-chain questionnaire, or an emerging market).
- For carbonTraceAI, the closest analogues are regulation-forced, document-heavy workflows: Prewave/osapiens (LkSG, CSDDD), Altana/Cervo (customs entries) and Paces (permit/interconnection submissions). Their human-in-the-loop "prepare and submit" pattern matches the CBAM declaration workflow.

- No public pricing was found for any agent product reviewed (Paces, PermitFlow, Workiva agents, Facilio, ARIA).
- No independent (non-vendor) measures of agent accuracy or ROI were found for any player.
- Datamaran, Interos, Gridware, Vaayu and Mavenoid were not researched in this pass.
- No funded startup focused specifically on agentic green-claims compliance (EU Green Claims / Empowering Consumers Directive) was found.

## What are the funding trends for climate tech and climate AI (2024, 2025, 2026), and what share goes to AI/software?

### Takeaway
Climate-tech venture funding was roughly flat to slightly up in 2025: about $40.5bn per Sightline/CTVC (+8%), or $42.2bn per PitchBook (-1.4%). H1 2026 rose about 55% to $26.1bn, but most of that growth came from low-carbon data-center and energy mega-rounds driven by AI power demand, not from AI software for sustainability. Deal counts fell every year, carbon/MRV funding collapsed, and no primary source gives a reliable "AI software share".

### Cited Findings
- **2025 (Sightline/CTVC):** $40.5bn climate venture and growth investment, up 8% from 2024. Deal count was 1,545, down 18% and the lowest since 2020. Seed fell 20% and Series A fell 7%, while growth-stage rose 78%. Energy took $14.4bn (36%), and mega-deals totalled $10.1bn, of which $7.2bn went to energy security. Europe fell 13% to $10.1bn and the US rose 27% — [Sightline/Currence 2025 report summary](https://www.currence.ai/blog/40-5bn-and-8-uptick-as-power-demand-drives-25-investment)
  - Note: sightlineclimate.com research URLs now redirect to currence.ai, and the CTVC H1'26 post credits "Currence". Sightline appears to have rebranded or merged, but no announcement was found.
- **2025 (PitchBook):** climate-tech VC deal value was $42.2bn, down 1.4% from $42.8bn in 2024. Deal count fell to a five-year low of 2,130 — [PitchBook Q4 2025 preview](https://pitchbook.brightspotcdn.com/99/96/badc1db34d4683341eb68a9fc740/q4-2025-climate-tech-vc-trends-preview.pdf)
- **Climate-specialist VC fund fundraising fell nearly 40% in 2025 versus 2024**, per PitchBook analyst John MacDonagh — [Fortune, Sept 23 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/09/23/climate-tech-vc-fundraising-is-down-almost-40-but-ai-is-driving-deals/). This conflicts with Sightline's figure of $103bn raised by new climate funds in 2025 ([Currence](https://www.currence.ai/blog/40-5bn-and-8-uptick-as-power-demand-drives-25-investment)); the gap is likely a difference in definitions (specialist VCs versus all climate funds, including infrastructure).
- **H1 2026 (CTVC/Currence):** $26.1bn, up 55% year on year and the strongest first half since 2022. Deal count fell 25% to a five-year low, and the top 10 deals took 42%. **Low-carbon data centers were 34% of H1 investment** (versus 3% a year earlier), and Built Environment grew more than 800%. Climate Management (the enterprise/carbon software category) rose only 6%. Carbon fell 61%, its weakest half since 2020. Series C hit a record $10.5bn. The biggest deals were DayOne ($4.5bn) and Nscale ($2bn) — [CTVC](https://www.ctvc.co/h126-climate-tech-funding-up-55-to-26bn-thanks-to-data-centers/)
- **H1 2026 (Dealroom):** $19.5B, with Q2 2026 at $10.4B, on a different methodology — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/guides/climate/)
- **Q1 2026 (PitchBook):** Europe led regional climate deal value for the first time on record, at $6.6B versus North America's $5.5B — [PitchBook Q1 2026 preview](https://pitchbook.brightspotcdn.com/e4/da/43dcc46e45b981a943163aaff6b0/q1-2026-climate-tech-vc-trends-preview.pdf)
- **AI-linked climate tech in 2025:** reportedly a record $6.6B across 304 deals, up 59% from 2024. The source is secondary and gives no dataset, so this is unverified and may be confused with the PitchBook Q1'26 Europe figure — [Superscout](https://superscout.co/sector/climate)
- **About 28% of climate equity dollars went to "AI-enabled solutions"** in 2025, attributed to Sightline. This was not found in Sightline's own summary and is unverified — [Green Collar Blog](https://greencollarblog.org/sightline-climate-2025-report/)
- Data centers took 78% of Built Environment funding in 2025, per Trellis's summary of Sightline — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/climate-tech-investment-2026-ai-wave/)
- Sightline itself raised $5.5M in January 2025 for an AI-powered climate market intelligence platform — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/01/27/sightline-secures-5-5m-for-climate-tech-analysis-platform/)

### Inferences
- In climate VC, "AI" mostly means capital for powering AI (data centers, firm power, grid). AI agents for sustainability workflows are a small slice. The category closest to ESG/carbon software (Climate Management) grew only 6% in H1 2026, while carbon markets fell 61%.
- Money is concentrating in later stages and bigger cheques, while seed and Series A deal counts fall. A seed-stage sustainability-agent startup faces a thinner climate-specialist investor pool (fund fundraising down about 40%). It may do better pitching itself to generalist AI/vertical-SaaS investors, which matches Earthshot's remark that 90% of its portfolio has generalist co-investors ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/23/climate-tech-vc-fundraising-is-down-almost-40-but-ai-is-driving-deals/)).

### Gaps
- No primary-source figure was found for the share of climate VC going to AI software or "AI agents" specifically. HolonIQ and BloombergNEF 2025/2026 figures were not retrieved.
- Full Sightline/Currence and PitchBook reports (paywalled) were not reviewed, so category-level software numbers are missing.
- The 2024 baseline is only implied (about $37.5bn by Sightline, back-calculated from +8%; $42.8bn by PitchBook). No standalone 2024 report was fetched.

## Which categories show real customer pull versus hype?

### Takeaway
The strongest pull is in permitting, interconnection and power development and in building operations. Both are backed by large rounds, strategic acquisitions and deployment numbers. Regulation-driven supply-chain due diligence and trade compliance also show real pull. ESG/CSRD reporting agents, carbon-credit MRV/rating and greenwashing agents look weaker or consolidating.

### Cited Findings
- **Pull — power/permitting:**
  - PermitFlow's $54M Series B was led by Accel ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251202551013/en/PermitFlow-Raises-$54-Million-to-Solve-Constructions-Biggest-Bottlenecks-With-AI)).
  - GridCARE's $64M Series A followed a $13.5M seed a year earlier ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260514546216/en/GridCARE-Raises-$64-Million-Series-A-from-Leading-AI-and-Energy-Investors-to-Create-a-New-Category-Power-Acceleration-for-AI)).
  - Paces Agent runs on more than 3.2 GW of pipeline, with customers EDF Renewables and AES ([Paces](https://www.paces.com/post/paces-agent-autonomous-ai-accelerated-power-development); [StartupIntros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/paces)).

### Inferences
- Pull is strongest where the agent unblocks revenue or capex: getting a power project or data center permitted and connected, or cutting HVAC energy costs. It is weaker where the agent only produces a disclosure whose regulatory scope is shrinking or uncertain, or where the underlying market (voluntary carbon) is contracting.
- Regulation-forced compliance with hard deadlines and penalties (customs/UFLPA, LkSG/CSDDD, and by analogy CBAM) produces real budgets. Buyers also tend to choose platforms that already hold supplier data, which favours incumbents and fast-moving "data network" players.
- Not verified in this pass: the 2025 EU "Omnibus" simplification, which narrowed CSRD and CSDDD scope, is likely a key reason CSRD-agent pull weakened. This needs a citation from the regulatory-research workstream.
