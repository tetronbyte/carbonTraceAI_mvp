# Adjacent Sustainability Regulations and Compliance Regimes (beyond EU CBAM), status as of 12 October 2026

Scope note: CBAM itself is out of scope (covered in `reports/CBAM agentic workflow market research.md`). These notes cover the regimes around it that create heavy compliance data work, with a lens on African and emerging-market exporters and on whether there is a task an agent could do. Many sources are law-firm summaries or vendor blogs. Where only a vendor blog supports a date, I flag it. Research ran 12 Oct 2026, and some files (EU Methane Regulation, SB 261, the Australian border adjustment) are moving week to week.

---

## 1. EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR): status, obligated parties, data burden, African smallholder impact, tooling

### Takeaway
After a second delay, the EUDR now applies from **30 Dec 2026 for large and medium operators** and **30 Jun 2027 for micro and small operators**. The April/May 2026 review left the core legal text alone, so plot-level geolocation and due diligence statements (DDS) still stand. West African cocoa (more than 90% smallholder-grown, mostly on informal tenure) is the most exposed supply chain in the world. Among the regimes in these notes, the EUDR has the strongest agent-shaped work, the largest African exposure, and the nearest deadline.

### Cited Findings
- **Timeline.** The Council adopted the targeted revision on 18 Dec 2025. It postpones application to 30 Dec 2026 (large/medium) and 30 Jun 2027 (micro/small), and the amendments took effect on 23 Dec 2025 after publication in the OJ — [Consilium press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/18/deforestation-council-signs-off-targeted-revision-to-simplify-and-postpone-the-regulation/); [EAS Project](https://easproject.com/eudr-delay/); [H2 Compliance](https://h2compliance.com/eudr-delay-2026-targeted-revision-new-compliance-timeline/). The amending act is cited as Regulation (EU) 2025/2650. One secondary source says the micro/small grace period covers firms already micro or small by 31 Dec 2024. Check this against the legal text.
- **Simplifications in the Dec 2025 revision.** Micro and small *primary* operators file one simplified declaration and receive a unique identifier, which they update only after major changes. Printed products were taken out of scope — [EAS Project](https://easproject.com/eudr-delay/); [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/eudr-delay-2025-what-eu-vote-means-for-importers-and-operators).
- **Commission review (due 30 Apr 2026).** It was published as report **COM(2026) 191, dated 4 May 2026**. The report covers how simplification has affected administrative burden, especially for micro and small operators, and how the Information System is progressing — [European Commission COM(2026)191 PDF](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf). Commentators say it does not reopen the core text and that Annex I (HS codes) will change by delegated act: cattle leather removed, palm-oil derivatives added. They also report that responsibility moves toward the first operator placing goods on the market, that downstream operators/traders register only to pass on DDS reference numbers, and that enforcement dates stay as they are — [Foods Connected](https://blog.foodsconnected.com/7-key-takeaways-from-the-april-2026-eudr-review); [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/eudr-simplification-2026); [Farmforce](https://farmforce.com/articles/the-eudr-simplification-review-lands-april-30-heres-what-it-actually-means-for-your-traceability-investments/). A June 2026 update says the Commission does not consider further amendments to the core text appropriate — [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/eudr-delay-2025-what-eu-vote-means-for-importers-and-operators).
- **Country benchmarking.** The first list (May 2025) put 140 countries in low risk, 50 in standard and 4 in high (Belarus, Myanmar, North Korea, Russia). A 2026 review based on FAO data was planned — [Preferred by Nature](https://www.preferredbynature.org/news/european-commission-publishes-first-list-country-benchmarks-under-eu-deforestation-regulation); [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/2-eudr-country-risk-classification-review-2026-what-changes-when-the-benchmarks-are-updated). Most major African cocoa and coffee origins (Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Cameroon, Nigeria, Uganda, Ethiopia) are understood to be "standard risk". I did not confirm the per-country tiers in this session (see Gaps).
- **TRACES / Information System.** Access was restricted from 16 Feb 2026 for maintenance, with reopening expected after mid-April 2026 — [osapiens](https://osapiens.com/blog/eudr-update-april-2026/).
- **Geolocation data burden.** A single point is enough for plots under 4 ha, and a polygon is required above that. Coordinates need at least 6 decimal places and must cover every plot that contributed to a batch, along with the harvest period. Farmer personal data is not required when the farmer is not a direct supplier — [EUDR Navigator](https://eudr-navigator.com/blog/eudr-cocoa-compliance-guide-en-us); [Meridia](https://www.meridia.land/blog/eudr-cocoa-compliance-a-comprehensive-guide). Land must not have been deforested after 31 Dec 2020 — [EUDR Navigator](https://eudr-navigator.com/blog/eudr-cocoa-compliance-guide-en-us).
- **African exposure.** More than 90% of West African cocoa comes from smallholders, many without GPS mapping or digital traceability. Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana typically produce more than 60% of world cocoa. Most cocoa grows on unregistered plots under informal tenure, and national mapping coverage is inadequate. Beans from many plots are mixed by intermediaries, and cocoa sourcing is roughly 50/50 direct versus indirect — [TraceX](https://tracextech.com/cocoa-traceability/); [TraceX smallholder data](https://tracextech.com/smallholder-data-for-cocoa-traceability/); [Meridia](https://www.meridia.land/blog/eudr-cocoa-compliance-a-comprehensive-guide).
- **Fairtrade deadlines.** Fairtrade extended its geolocation deadline to Jan 2027 for coffee cooperatives worldwide and for LatAm/Caribbean cocoa, but **not for African and Asian cocoa**, which stay on the earlier schedule — [Fairtrade](https://www.fairtrade.net/en/fairtrade-extends-timeline-for-cooperatives-to-complete-geolocation-data-collection.html).
- **Coffee readiness.** Geo-referencing and farm mapping are coffee's strongest readiness areas, but smallholder dominance limits data quality and digital capacity — [FoodNavigator, Apr 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/04/17/eudr-how-ready-is-coffee/).
- **Certification is not enough on its own.** Certification data can be imported and cross-referenced, but plot GPS is still needed — [Meridia](https://www.meridia.land/blog/eudr-cocoa-compliance-a-comprehensive-guide).
- **Commodities in scope** (relevant to Africa): cocoa, coffee, cattle, timber, palm oil, rubber, soy, plus derivatives such as chocolate, leather, furniture and paper — [allAfrica (Uganda), Feb 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602040440.html).
- **Evidence of pain (advocacy figures, unverified methodology).** A Feb 2026 regional-dialogue report claims smallholders may spend "up to 75% of their annual income on compliance", that up to 85% of East African agribusinesses lack substantive knowledge of the requirements, and that the measures are "already reducing African exports" (cases in Nigeria and Cameroon) — [allAfrica (Uganda): Green Deal or Green Wall?](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602040440.html). A University of Pretoria/GIBS study finds EU regulatory complexity is "a significant challenge" for African agricultural exporters, mainly small-scale ones — [UP Repository](https://repository.up.ac.za/items/f36db27d-277e-4139-a9cc-9b5e65ad4663/full).
- **Tool market.** A June 2026 vendor ranking lists osapiens, Mindsprint TruTrace, IntegrityNext, Sourcemap, SourceTrace and Coolset as leading EUDR tools. It rates SourceTrace "best for agri-origin traceability in emerging markets and smallholder networks". Coolset starts at about EUR 12k/yr and most others quote custom pricing — [Coolset (vendor, self-interested)](https://www.coolset.com/academy/best-6-eudr-compliance-tools-for-2026-supply-chain-due-diligence). Other players include [IntegrityNext](https://www.integritynext.com/esg-solutions/eudr-deforestation-regulation), [Tesisquare](https://www.tesisquare.com/en/eudr-compliance-software-platform/), [Tanso](https://www.tanso.de/en/sustainable-supply-chain/eudr), [Mindsprint](https://www.mindsprint.com/resources/blogs/eudr-software), [Farmforce](https://farmforce.com/) and [Meridia](https://www.meridia.land/). On origin-side funding: Koltiva (Indonesia) raised an undisclosed Series A in Sep 2023 led by AC Ventures and claims 1M+ producers and 6,800 businesses — [TNGlobal](https://technode.global/2023/09/19/indonesian-sustainable-farming-and-supply-chain-traceability-startup-koltiva-raises-series-a-financing-led-by-ac-ventures/). Farmforce raised about $3M in a Series A in Jun 2025, led by Edaphon (aggregator data, figures differ) — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/farmforce); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/farmforce).

### Inferences
- The EUDR is the closest analogue to CBAM. Data is requested by EU importers and has to be produced by non-EU origin actors (cooperatives, licensed buying companies, exporters), and deadlines are near: 30 Dec 2026 for most buyers. Agent-shaped tasks:
  1. Ingest messy cooperative registers (Excel, paper scans, WhatsApp photos) and normalise them into plot/farmer/batch records.
  2. Validate geometry (≥6 decimals, polygon for plots over 4 ha, self-intersections, overlap with protected areas or deforestation alerts after 2020).
  3. Reconcile batch mass balance to plots, which is the hard part with intermediated, commingled cocoa.
  4. Map certification evidence (Rainforest Alliance, Fairtrade) to EUDR fields.
  5. Assemble DDS evidence packs and TRACES reference-number chains.
  6. Draft legality (land tenure, labour, tax) evidence in each origin country's legal framework.
- The well-funded tool market sits mostly on the **EU buyer side** (osapiens, IntegrityNext, Coolset) or in **field-mapping apps**. The middle layer of African exporter/cooperative "evidence factories" that sell into several EU buyers looks thinner, but this is an inference from the vendor lists, not a measured market gap.
- The Dec 2025 amendment's push of obligations toward the first placer, plus the simplified declaration for micro/small operators in low-risk countries, mostly benefits EU-side SMEs. Origin-side data production in standard-risk African countries does not get lighter.

### Gaps
- Per-country benchmarking tiers for African origins, and whether the planned 2026 benchmarking review has been published. Not confirmed.
- Whether COM(2026)191 created any "no/negligible-risk" category. Commentators speculated about one, but I could not confirm it.
- No reliable per-farmer or per-tonne cost figures for EUDR mapping in Côte d'Ivoire or Ghana. The 75%-of-income figure is an advocacy estimate.
- Status of the national traceability systems in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana (CCC/COCOBOD) as of Oct 2026, which matters for whether a SaaS layer competes with or complements state systems. Not researched.

## 2. CSRD/ESRS after Omnibus I, CSDDD changes, and the VSME/"VS" value-chain cap

### Takeaway
Omnibus I is now law: Directive (EU) 2026/470, published 26 Feb 2026 and in force 18 Mar 2026. CSRD now covers only companies with >1,000 employees **and** >EUR 450m turnover. CSDDD covers >5,000 employees and >EUR 1.5bn, applying from 26 Jul 2029. A **value-chain cap** protects suppliers with ≤1,000 employees, under which large reporters may not ask for more than the voluntary standard "VS" (formerly VSME) for financial years from 1 Jan 2027. For African SME suppliers, this turns open-ended ESG questionnaires into a bounded, standardised data request, which is a good target for a "fill once, share many" agent product.

### Cited Findings
- **Legislative path.** Parliament approved on 16 Dec 2025 and the Council adopted on 24 Feb 2026. The act was published in the OJ on 26 Feb 2026 as Directive (EU) 2026/470 and entered into force on 18 Mar 2026. CSRD amendments must be transposed by 19 Mar 2027 and CSDDD by 26 Jul 2028, with CSDDD compliance from 26 Jul 2029 — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/02/eu-csddd-csrd-omnibus-published-in-official-journal-transposition-delegated-acts-and-guidelines-are-next); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/business-and-human-rights-insights/2026/02/omnibus-i-the-european-union-concludes-csddd-and-csrd-reforms.html); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/lux/legal-updates/final-adoption-of-omnibus-i-directive-simplifying-csrd-and-csddd).
- **CSRD thresholds.** EU undertakings must exceed both >1,000 employees (average over the financial year) and >EUR 450m net turnover. Non-EU groups are in scope with >EUR 450m EU turnover for each of the last two years plus an EU subsidiary or branch with >EUR 200m. Member States may exempt sub-threshold companies for FY2025–2026 — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/agreement-on-the-csrd-cs3d-omnibus-package-key-changes-and-implications); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/lux/legal-updates/final-adoption-of-omnibus-i-directive-simplifying-csrd-and-csddd); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251222-eu-sustainability-omnibus-i-detailed-omnibus).
- **CSDDD thresholds.** EU companies need 5,000+ employees and >EUR 1.5bn worldwide turnover. Non-EU companies need >EUR 1.5bn EU turnover, with no employee test. Penalties are capped at 3% of worldwide net turnover — [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/omnibus-i-clarity-on-the-future-of-the-csrd-and-csddd); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/business-and-human-rights-insights/2026/02/omnibus-i-the-european-union-concludes-csddd-and-csrd-reforms.html).
- **Value-chain cap.** "Protected undertakings" have ≤1,000 employees. In-scope companies may not request more than the voluntary standard requires, and suppliers may refuse excessive requests — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/agreement-on-the-csrd-cs3d-omnibus-package-key-changes-and-implications).
- **Voluntary Standard (VS, formerly VSME).** Adopted as delegated regulation C(2026) 5011 on 3 Jul 2026 and published in the OJ on 21 Sep 2026 as Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1560, it entered into force on 24 Sep 2026. The cap applies to FYs beginning on or after 1 Jan 2027 — [FIEC](https://www.fiec.eu/news/news-2026/simplified-sustainability-reporting-standards-published-eu-official-journal); [ESGDO](https://esgdo.com/news/vsme-voluntary-standard-scrutiny-ends-september-2026); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/speaking-sustainability/eu-finalizes-streamlined-esrs-and-new-voluntary-reporting-standard); [EFRAG](https://www.efrag.org/en/news-and-calendar/news/european-commission-publishes-delegated-act-on-revised-esrs-and-voluntary-sustainability-reporting). The VSME has been renamed "VS" — [Ditto](https://www.trustditto.com/en/collection/vsme/vsme-renamed-vs-what-changes). The cap does **not** stop bank questionnaires, tender criteria or voluntary sector surveys — [Fiegenbaum Solutions](https://www.fiegenbaum.solutions/en/blog/eu-value-chain-cap-five-gaps-voluntary-standard).
- **Revised ESRS.** Adopted 3 Jul 2026 and published in the OJ 21 Sep 2026 as Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1563, they enter into force 10 Nov 2026 and apply to FYs from 1 Jan 2027, with optional early use for FY2026 by wave one. Mandatory datapoints are cut by more than 60% and total datapoints by more than 70% — [FIEC](https://www.fiec.eu/news/news-2026/simplified-sustainability-reporting-standards-published-eu-official-journal); [PwC Viewpoint](https://viewpoint.pwc.com/gx/en/pwc/in-briefs/2026/ib_int202617.html); [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/csrd-technical-resources/eu-adopts-revised-esrs-for-sustainability-reporting); [Deloitte DART](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/publications/deloitte/heads-up/2026/eu-sustainability-reporting-omnibus-esrs-revised). Some sources give conflicting adoption and publication dates (e.g. Ebner Stolz). The 3 Jul adoption / 21 Sep publication is the consensus.

### Inferences
- **Direct CSRD/CSDDD obligation on African firms is now negligible.** Only very large non-EU groups (>EUR 450m EU turnover) are caught. The relevant exposure is **indirect**: EU customers' Scope 3 and CSDDD due-diligence requests flow down to African suppliers.
- **The VS standard creates a defined data product.** An agent could pre-fill a supplier's VS module from invoices, utility bills, payroll and fuel logs, keep it current, and answer each customer's request "up to the cap". That would be a defensible "supplier passport" sitting naturally next to CBAM/EUDR data for the same manufacturer. The product angle could include "Refuse excessive request" letters that cite the cap.
- Pain persists outside the cap (bank ESG questionnaires, tenders, CDP Supply Chain), so a multi-format questionnaire-answering agent backed by one evidence store keeps its value.
- With CSDDD compliance now 2029 and thresholds at 5,000 employees, CSDDD-driven supplier pressure on Africa will build slowly. EUDR, the Forced Labour Regulation and the Battery Regulation are nearer-term drivers.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the status of the "stop-the-clock" deferral for wave-2/3 CSRD reporters, or how Omnibus I treats listed SMEs. Both affect demand on the EU buyer side.
- Exact VS module contents (Basic and Comprehensive datapoint counts) not retrieved.
- No data on how many EU buyers have actually limited supplier questionnaires to VS so far.

## 3. Product regimes: ESPR Digital Product Passports, EU Battery Regulation, PPWR, Green Claims, Empowering Consumers (EmpCo)

### Takeaway
These regimes are product-data compliance: passports, declarations of conformity and claim substantiation. They are live or near-term:
- Battery passport: **18 Feb 2027**.
- PPWR: applied **12 Aug 2026**.
- EmpCo: applied **27 Sep 2026**.
- Battery due diligence: postponed to **18 Aug 2027**.
- ESPR product acts: steel first (2026 target, about 2028 compliance), textiles in 2027.

The **Green Claims Directive is blocked, not formally withdrawn**. The most relevant for African exporters are battery due diligence (critical minerals: DRC, Zambia, Zimbabwe), PPWR (any packaged export to the EU) and ESPR steel/textiles (Morocco/Tunisia/Egypt/Ethiopia/Kenya apparel; North/South African steel).

### Cited Findings
- **ESPR working plan.** Adopted 16 Apr 2025 as COM(2025) 187. Indicative delegated-act years: iron & steel 2026; textiles, tyres and aluminium 2027; furniture 2028; mattresses 2029. A mid-term review is due in 2028 — [PassportCraft](https://passportcraft.com/insights/espr-timeline-what-brands-need-to-know); [DigiProductPass](https://www.digiproductpass.com/espr-work-plan-2025-2030); [dpp-tool](https://dpp-tool.com/en/blog/espr-delegated-acts/). One tracker (around Aug 2026) says **no product-specific ESPR delegated acts had been adopted yet** — [dpp-tool](https://dpp-tool.com/en/blog/espr-delegated-acts/). Steel compliance is estimated around 2028 (18-month transition), and textile passports in late 2028 to 2029 — [MyProductPassport](https://myproductpassport.eu/blog/espr-timeline-and-deadlines-when-does-your-sector-need-to-comply); [Renoon](https://www.renoon.com/blog/delegated-acts-for-digital-product-passport-explainer-and-current-status). (Vendor sources, dates indicative.)
- **ESPR destruction ban.** The ban on destroying unsold apparel and footwear has applied to large companies since 19 Jul 2026 and applies to medium companies from 19 Jul 2030 — [PassportCraft](https://passportcraft.com/insights/dpp-timeline-2026-2030-every-deadline).
- **Battery passport.** Mandatory from **18 Feb 2027** for EV, LMT and industrial batteries above 2 kWh, via QR code. The first passport needs only basic identification and technical data — [Environment+Energy Leader](https://environmentenergyleader.com/stories/battery-passport-becomes-mandatory-february-18-2027,142747); [Circularise](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/eu-battery-passport-regulation-requirements).
- **Battery due diligence postponed.** Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 amended Art. 48, moving due diligence from 18 Aug 2025 to **18 Aug 2027**, with Commission guidance due 26 Jul 2026 — [Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/eu-battery-regulation-for-importers/); [Asuene](https://asuene.com/us/blog/eu-battery-regulation-2027-executive-readiness-for-carbon-footprint-battery-passport-and-due-diligence). One vendor says the obligation applies only above **EUR 150m** annual net turnover (single vendor source, unverified) — [Asuene](https://asuene.com/us/blog/eu-battery-regulation-2027-executive-readiness-for-carbon-footprint-battery-passport-and-due-diligence).
- **Battery carbon footprint declaration is stalled.** The methodology delegated act, originally due by Feb 2025, has been delayed. A vendor cites a Commission guidance document of 15 Aug 2026 saying the carbon-footprint declaration, label and recycled-content fields are "not to be filled/displayed as of February 2027" pending the acts. **Unverified against a primary source** — [Cirtrace](https://cirtrace.com/blog/en/posts/battery-carbon-footprint-declaration-2027-guide). Sources conflict: some claim EV battery declarations have applied since 18 Feb 2025 — [battery.mba](https://www.battery.mba/resources/eu-battery-regulation).
- **PPWR (Reg. (EU) 2025/40).** Applied 12 Aug 2026 with no grace period for earlier-produced stock. Importers must verify manufacturer compliance (material composition, restricted substances, recyclability, minimisation), and non-EU firms may need an EU authorised representative. PFAS limits apply to food-contact packaging from 12 Aug 2026. Harmonised labelling follows from 12 Aug 2028 (or later), and recyclability grades A–C, plastic recycled content and the 50% empty-space cap from 1 Jan 2030 — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-summary-of-provisions-and-new-guidance); [Gerlach](https://gerlach-customs.com/news/news-and-trends/ppwr-2026-packaging-rules-eu-importers/); [UK business.gov.uk](https://www.business.gov.uk/campaign/europe/european-union-eu-regulations/eu-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-eu-ppwr/); [ppwrcopilot](https://ppwrcopilot.com/blog/ppwr-august-2026-compliance-checklist).
- **Green Claims Directive.** The Commission announced its intent to withdraw on 20 Jun 2025 and the trilogue was cancelled on 23 Jun 2025. EVP Ribera then said on 25 Jun that it had not officially been withdrawn. The Parliament Legislative Train lists it as "Blocked", with the Council as blocker, and the Commission's 2026 work programme still lists it as pending — [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102lrh5/eu-sustainability-initiatives-in-the-commission-2026-work-programme); [Matheson](https://www.mhc.ie/latest/insights/greenwashing-update-on-the-green-claims-directive); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/aut/legal-updates/the-eu-green-claims-directive-where-are-we-now-and-what-s-next).
- **EmpCo (Directive (EU) 2024/825).** Transposition was due 27 Mar 2026 and the directive applies from **27 Sep 2026**. It bans generic environmental claims and offset-based "carbon neutral" claims, with no transition period for existing stock. The Commission opened infringements against 20 Member States on 28 May 2026 for incomplete transposition — [ClimatePartner](https://www.climatepartner.com/en/knowledge/blog/empco-directive-eu-implementation-status-and-what-companies-need-to-know); [Certivo](https://www.certivo.com/blog-details/eu-empowering-consumers-directive-green-claims-rules-from-september-2026); [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/consumers/consumer-rights-and-complaints/sustainable-consumption_en).

### Inferences
- DPP and PPWR work is document- and bill-of-materials-heavy: declarations of conformity, supplier material declarations, substance test reports and recycled-content certificates. Agent-shaped tasks include extracting and normalising supplier declarations, gap-checking against article requirements, and generating DoC/technical-file packs. For African exporters, PPWR is **already live** and touches every packaged food, beverage or cosmetic export (e.g. Kenyan tea and horticulture, South African wine, Moroccan food). It is probably under-served on the exporter side, though this is an inference with no market data found.
- Battery due diligence (Aug 2027) and the battery passport (Feb 2027) push upstream into African cobalt/copper/lithium/graphite supply (DRC, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Namibia) through OECD-style mineral due diligence. This is an evidence-pack problem much like CBAM's. The regulation's mineral list and the African upstream link come from background knowledge and were not verified this session.
- For an exporter, EmpCo mainly affects marketing copy on EU shelves. A "claim checker" agent is a small adjacent feature, not a core wedge, for an Africa-focused manufacturer tool.
- The carbon-footprint methodology delay for batteries means product carbon footprints (PCF, under the PEF methodology) are not yet locked. CBAM-style embedded-emissions engines could be extended to PCF once the rules settle.

### Gaps
- No primary Commission source confirming the 15 Aug 2026 battery guidance or the EUR 150m due-diligence threshold.
- No confirmation that the ESPR steel delegated act has been adopted or proposed as of Oct 2026.
- No data on PPWR enforcement or rejection of non-EU shipments since 12 Aug 2026.

## 4. EU Methane Regulation (Reg. (EU) 2024/1787) import requirements, relevance to African oil, gas and LNG exporters

### Takeaway
The legal text still requires EU importers to show **MRV equivalence from 1 Jan 2027** for oil, gas and coal under contracts concluded or renewed from 4 Aug 2024. The regime is unravelling, though. The Commission recommended **no fines for 2027–2029** (20 Jul 2026), and on **28 Sep 2026 it confirmed it will propose postponing** the import rules, with one year under examination. Nigeria LNG is Africa's most advanced producer (OGMP 2.0 Level 5, DNV-assured). Algeria and Sonatrach are the most exposed. Agent-shaped work exists (producer MRV evidence, contract clause management, commingled-cargo attribution), but buyers are large and few, and the date is likely to slip.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal requirement.** From 1 Jan 2027, importers must show equivalent MRV for oil, gas and coal under contracts concluded or renewed from 4 Aug 2024, and "all reasonable efforts" for older contracts. Methane-intensity reporting starts Aug 2028, and a crude-oil intensity methodology delegated act is due by 5 Aug 2027 — [European Commission – import requirements](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/methane-regulation-import-requirements_en); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/eu-methane-regulation-application-lng-coal-mine-operators-importers/). Penalties can reach 20% of annual turnover — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-industry/2906-56881-algeria-nigeria-join-us-qatar-in-call-for-eu-methane-rule-revision).
- **Commission recommendations, 20 Jul 2026 (non-binding).** One recommends Member States refrain from fining importers for 2027–2029. The other offers optional model contract clauses. Obligations remain in force — [GlobalELR](https://www.globalelr.com/2026/07/eu-methane-regulation-european-commission-recommends-delaying-penalties/); [Mayer Brown, Aug 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/regulation-of-methane-emissions-in-the-oil-and-gas-industry-european-commission-recommendation-and-brazils-progress-on-the-regulatory-agenda).
- **Planned postponement (29 Sep 2026).** A Commission spokesperson said: "The Commission plans to propose a postponement of the entry into force of the methane regulation". Commissioner Jørgensen indicated one year is under examination, and the length is not confirmed. France, Germany and about a dozen other governments backed a delay or suspension, and the US also pushed — [Brussels Morning, 29 Sep 2026](https://brusselsmorning.com/eu-methane-emissions-rules-delay/104373/). Industry (11 European players, Eurogas joint paper of Apr 2026) asked for a conditional, time-bound deferral of Arts 28–29 — [Agence Europe](https://agenceurope.eu/en/bulletin/article/13852/12/methane-regulation-eleven-european-oil-and-gas-players-call-for-postponement-of-planned-equivalence-requirements-to-1-january-2027); [Eurogas joint paper](https://www.eurogas.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Joint-industry-amendment-proposals-for-EUMR-importer-requirements.pdf).
- **Feasibility.** An IOGP-commissioned study (Mar 2026, producer perspective) finds no exporting country fully meets the Art. 28 equivalence criteria and no standardised Level-5 verification protocol exists. It estimates up to 87% of EU crude and 43% of gas imports could be non-compliant if the rules are applied as written, and notes that commingling, blending and portfolio trading block attribution — [IOGP Europe study summary](https://iogpeurope.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/EU-Methane-Emissions-Regulation-Study-Summary-Report.pdf).
- **African producers.** In June 2026, Algeria (Arkab) and Nigeria (Ekpo) joined Qatar and the US in asking the EU to revise the MRV deadlines — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-industry/2906-56881-algeria-nigeria-join-us-qatar-in-call-for-eu-methane-rule-revision). NLNG claims to be first in Africa at OGMP 2.0 Level 5, with MRV assured by DNV to ISO 14064 — [allAfrica, Sep 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202609180607.html). Sonatrach has reportedly not joined OGMP 2.0 and Algeria did not sign the Global Methane Pledge — [Datadesk](https://blog.datadesk.eco/p/algeria-methane); [Datadesk EUMR supply analysis](https://research.datadesk.eco/eumr-supply/). An Oct 2026 African commentary argues a pause is "no free pass" for African producers — [Africa Oil & Gas Report](https://africaoilgasreport.com/2026/10/in-the-news/eu-methane-equivalent-rules-calls-for-a-pause-is-no-free-pass-for-african-hydrocarbon-producers-not-to-act/).
- **Certification route.** MiQ says it can certify a producer within 2–6 months of starting (vendor claim) — [MiQ](https://miq.org/thought-leadership/meeting-the-eu-methane-regulations-crude-oil-import-requirements-with-miq-certification/).

### Inferences
- This is a big-ticket but **narrow-customer** market: IOCs, NOCs, LNG traders and EU utilities. The tasks fit agents well: parsing MRV and OGMP reports, mapping producer evidence to Art. 28 criteria, contract-clause redlining with model clauses, and cargo-to-producer attribution under commingling. But buyers are incumbents with Big-4 or measurement vendors, and the timeline is likely to slip by about a year.
- For an SME-focused manufacturer tool like carbonTraceAI, the Methane Regulation is a **poor near-term fit** except possibly as a consulting-style wedge with mid-size African upstream independents.

### Gaps
- Mozambique LNG, Angola and Egypt MRV status were not found.
- Whether the Commission's postponement proposal has been tabled, and its exact length, is not confirmed as of 12 Oct 2026.

## 5. Other EU regimes: Forced Labour Regulation, ETS2, FuelEU Maritime / ETS maritime, ReFuelEU Aviation / CORSIA

### Takeaway
The **Forced Labour Regulation (FLR)** applies from **14 Dec 2027** and is the most relevant to African exporters. It is a product ban with no size threshold that covers imports. Commission guidelines came out on 26 Jun 2026, and the risk database is uncertain. **ETS2 is postponed to 1 Jan 2028**, an EU-internal fuel-supplier regime with little relevance to African exporters. **Maritime** (ETS at 100% surrender for 2026 emissions; FuelEU in its second year) and **aviation** (ReFuelEU 2% SAF met in 2025; CORSIA Phase 1 offsets due 31 Jan 2028) are data-heavy but concentrated in shipping and aviation operators. They touch African carriers and ports only at the margin.

### Cited Findings
- **FLR (Reg. (EU) 2024/3015).** Applies from 14 Dec 2027. Member States must notify penalty rules by 14 Dec 2026 — [osapiens](https://osapiens.com/en/regulations/eu-forced-labor-regulation); [TrusTrace](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ban-on-forced-labor-regulation-proposal); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102jrun/eu-forced-labor-regulation-published-in-official-journal-beginning-the-countdown). Commission guidelines were published 26 Jun 2026 (non-binding but citable) — [Baker McKenzie](https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/08/24/eu-publishes-guidelines-on-the-application-of-the-eu-forced-labour-regulation/); [Sustainability in Business blog](https://www.sustainabilityinbusiness.blog/2026/07/european-commission-publishes-its-guidelines-on-the-eu-forced-labour-regulation/); [AGRINFO](https://agrinfo.eu/book-of-reports/guidelines-on-the-eu-forced-labour-regulation/). **Conflict:** TrusTrace says the forced-labour risk database launched with the guidelines, but Dutch RVO says it is not yet available — [TrusTrace](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ban-on-forced-labor-regulation-proposal) vs [RVO](https://english.rvo.nl/topics/forced-labour-regulation). Mayer Brown published a compliance roadmap in Sep 2026 — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/a-practical-roadmap-for-compliance-with-the-eu-forced-labour-regulation).
- **ETS2.** Ministers agreed a one-year delay in Nov 2025, folded into the revised Climate Law (2040 target of −90%). Parliament voted 413–226–12, start is now 1 Jan 2028, and auctioning still begins in 2027 — [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-officially-adopts-2040-climate-target-postpones-ets-2-one-year); [EU Perspectives](https://euperspectives.eu/2026/02/plenary-approved-2040-climate-target-ets2-postponed-until-2028/); [BUILD UP (EC)](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/en/news-and-events/news/eu-postpones-carbon-pricing-buildings-2028-climate-law-deal). cep argues the postponement procedure is legally problematic — [cep](https://www.cep.eu/eu-topics/details/legal-problems-of-the-ets-2-postponement.html).
- **ETS maritime.** Surrender was 40% for 2024 and 70% for 2025 emissions, and **100% from 2026 emissions**, with CH4 and N2O now in scope — [OceanScore](https://oceanscore.com/insights/maritime-compliance-requirements-2026-eu-ets-fueleu-uk-ets/); [Gasum](https://www.gasum.com/en/news-and-customer-stories/articles/2026/eu-ets-and-fueleu-maritime-the-double-dividend-of-lng-compliance-in-2026/).
- **FuelEU Maritime.** The first reporting year closed 31 Dec 2025, with verification and pooling due 30 Apr 2026 and penalties around 30 Jun 2026. The penalty is EUR 2,400/t VLSFO-equivalent. Coverage is 100% of intra-EU voyage energy and 50% for EU–non-EU voyages — [GreenFuelJournal](https://www.greenfueljournal.com/post/fueleu-maritime-2026-2027); [apeks.tech](https://apeks.tech/insights/fueleu-maritime-compliance-strategy/). (Secondary sources, minor date inconsistencies.)
- **ReFuelEU Aviation (Reg. (EU) 2023/2405).** The 2025 SAF mandate was 2%, and the actual figure was 2.79% (1.1 Mt of 39.3 Mt). 90% of fuel suppliers and 93% of aircraft operators reported. The 6% target for 2030 looks reachable per EASA — [EASA ReFuelEU Annual Technical Report 2026](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/general-publications/refueleu-aviation-annual-technical-report-2026); [EASA press release](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/newsroom-and-events/press-releases/easa-publishes-report-sustainable-aviation-fuel-scale-progress).
- **CORSIA.** Phase 1 (2024–26) offsets must be cancelled by 31 Jan 2028, against a baseline of 85% of 2019 emissions. 130 states volunteered, with an exemption below 3,000 tCO2 of offset requirement. About 41M eligible units exist against needs up to 4x that. Phase 2 (2027–35) is mandatory for most states — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ee86d6c7/corsia-a-market-update); [EDF, Jul 2026](https://blogs.edf.org/energyexchange/2026/07/27/corsia-at-a-crossroads-the-next-year-will-determine-its-course/); [Carbon Direct](https://www.carbon-direct.com/insights/corsia-phase-1-credits-compliance-and-what-comes-next).

### Inferences
- **The FLR is an exporter-side evidence problem for Africa.** Cocoa (child/forced-labour risk in West Africa), cotton, mining (DRC artisanal cobalt) and fishing all face a ban-based regime where authorities investigate on risk signals. Exporters will need defensible labour due-diligence dossiers (audits, grievance logs, recruitment-fee evidence, supplier mapping). This fits an agent well (document ingestion, risk-indicator matching against guideline criteria, evidence-pack generation) and overlaps heavily with EUDR data for the same cocoa and coffee supply chains, which could make one origin evidence platform. The point that the FLR has no company-size threshold and covers products made anywhere is background knowledge from the regulation text and was not re-verified this session.
- ETS2, maritime and aviation are operator regimes with mature incumbent tooling (shipping MRV platforms, airline fuel systems). They are weak fits for an African-manufacturer SaaS. CORSIA has an Africa angle only through supplying carbon credits (Article 6 / CORSIA-eligible units from African projects), which is a different business.

### Gaps
- Whether the FLR database or Single Portal is live as of Oct 2026 (conflicting sources).
- No data on African carriers' or ports' FuelEU exposure.

## 6. UK CBAM and other border carbon / carbon-pricing schemes (Canada, Australia, Japan, China, India, Türkiye, South Africa)

### Takeaway
The **UK CBAM starts 1 Jan 2027**. It covers aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, and iron & steel (no electricity; glass and ceramics excluded for now), with a **£50k** import-value threshold, and the first return and payment fall due by 31 May 2028. It is a direct CBAM clone where carbonTraceAI's engine could be reused for the same African exporters (e.g. South African, Egyptian or Mozambican steel, aluminium and fertiliser to the UK). Elsewhere:
- **Australia** is weighing a phased BCA starting with cement and clinker, with no decision before the 2026–27 Safeguard review.
- **Canada** has no live federal BCA workstream (contested).
- Domestic ETSs are tightening: **Japan GX-ETS** mandatory since Apr 2026, **China ETS** expanded to steel, cement and aluminium, **India CCTS** first compliance year closed, **Türkiye ETS** pilot (reporting-only in 2026), and **South Africa** carbon tax Phase 2 at R308/t.

These shape CBAM deduction claims (carbon price paid) and create in-country MRV work.

### Cited Findings
- **UK CBAM.** Starts 1 Jan 2027 and was legislated via Finance Bill 2025/26 (cited as Finance Act 2026 Part 5, Sch. 17, which I did not verify on legislation.gov.uk). Sectors are aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen and iron & steel. Electricity is excluded, and glass and ceramics are temporarily excluded. Some scrap is excluded by commodity code. Indirect emissions are deferred until 2029 at the earliest — [Deloitte Taxscape](https://taxscape.deloitte.com/measures-autumn-budget-2025/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism.aspx); [RSM UK](https://www.rsmuk.com/insights/tax-voice/are-you-ready-for-uk-cbam-implementation-in-2027). The threshold was raised from £10k to **£50k**, tested on a rolling 12-month look-back or an expected 30-day look-ahead. The liable party is the UK importer. The first return and payment are due by 31 May 2028 — [CBAM Journal](https://www.cbamjournal.com/post/uk-cbam-registration-hmrc-50000-threshold); [IACBAM](https://www.iacbam.org/updates/uk-hmrc-cbam-registration-guidance); [Carbmee](https://www.carbmee.com/knowledge-insights/blog-article/uk-cbam-explained).
- **Australia.** The Carbon Leakage Review final report recommends a phased border measure: cement and clinker first; lime, hydrogen, ammonia, steel, iron and glass second. It rejects export rebates. The government will consider it in the 2026–27 Safeguard Mechanism review, and NZ would move "in conjunction with Australia" — [PwC Australia](https://www.pwc.com.au/tax/tax-alerts/final-report-from-the-australian-carbon-leakage-review.html); [Clayton Utz, Aug 2026](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2026/august/carbon-border-adjustments-compliance-and-commercial-implications-for-australian-businesses); [The Eco Ledger](https://theecoledger.com/insights/australia-nz-cbam-decision-window). Sources date the review differently (Mar 2025 vs Feb 2026).
- **Canada.** Chambers says the BCA consultation has been archived and there appears to be no live federal workstream — [Chambers Climate Change Regulation 2026](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/climate-change-regulation-2026/17/). IISD's *State of Border Carbon Adjustments 2026* exists but I could not read its contents — [IISD](https://www.iisd.org/system/files/2026-07/state-of-border-carbon-adjustments-2026.pdf).
- **Japan GX-ETS.** Mandatory from FY2026 (Apr 2026) for emitters with >100k tCO2 Scope 1, which is about 300–400 companies and roughly 60% of national CO2. Formal trading is expected from autumn 2027 — [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/ets/japan-gx-ets); [Asuene](https://asuene.com/us/blog/why-data-integrity-will-define-success-under-japans-gx-ets); [CarbonMeld](https://carbonmeld.com/en/articles/japan-gx-ets-trading-design-who-can-trade/).
- **China ETS.** The MEE work plan of 20 Mar 2025 added cement, steel and aluminium (including CF4 and C2F6 for aluminium). First compliance was end-2025 for 2024 emissions, with free allocation equal to verified emissions (familiarisation year). Allocation is intensity-based with no absolute cap, and benchmarks tighten from 2027 — [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/china-officially-expands-national-ets-cement-steel-and-aluminum-sectors); [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/blog/energy-transition/040225-et-highlights-china-expands-compliance-carbon-market-steel-aluminum-cement); [Sino-German Climate Cooperation](https://climatecooperation.cn/climate/china-releases-allocation-plan-for-the-national-emissions-trading-scheme-for-the-steel-cement-and-aluminium-industries/).
- **India CCTS.** The first compliance year ran 1 Apr 2025 to 31 Mar 2026, the first filing deadline was 31 Jul 2026, and the next is 31 Jul 2027. Iron & steel targets were still a draft (G.S.R. 517(E), 26 Jun 2026). Trading is "expected" around Oct 2026 but not legally committed — [CarbonNeeti](https://carbonneeti.com/blog/india-ccts-compliance-deadlines-2026); [CBAM Guide](https://cbamguide.com/news/2026-07-20-india-ccts-carbon-market-cbam-recognition/); [InfluenceMap](https://india.influencemap.org/policy/Carbon-Credit-Trading-Scheme-CCTS-465).
- **Türkiye ETS.** Pilot rules were published 1 Oct 2026, covering 2026–27 emissions. 2026 is reporting-only (voluntary payment possible). Free allocation is 100% for 2027 and 2027 surrender is due end-Nov 2028. The threshold is >50k t/yr installations, with the full phase from 2028 — [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/turkiye-issues-implementation-rules-tr-ets-pilot-phase); [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/559454/).
- **South Africa carbon tax Phase 2.** Started 1 Jan 2026 at R308/t, up from R236, rising to R462 by 2030 (R347 in 2027, R385 in 2028, R424 in 2029). The Taxation Laws Amendment Act No. 5 of 2026 (published 1 Apr 2026) introduces financial consequences for exceeding carbon budgets. The offset allowance rises to 15% (combustion) and 10% (process/fugitive). **Conflict:** PwC says the basic allowance is retained to 2030, while another outlet says it falls from 60% to 50% — [PwC Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/corporate/significant-developments); [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/za/insights/south-african-carbon-tax-update-2026/); [Yellow Tree](https://yellowtree.co.za/taxation-laws-amendment-act-2026/); [SolarXGen](https://solarxgen.co.za/insights/carbon-tax-phase-2-is-now-live-why-the-r308-tonne-rate-and-shrinking-allowances-make-solar-and-bess-the-most-effective-compliance-tool-for-c-i-energy-users-in-2026).
- **CBAM deductions.** A carbon price already paid in the country of production can be deducted from the CBAM obligation — [BLG / Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/tax-authorities/1731660/the-worlds-first-carbon-border-adjusted-tax-enters-its-definitive-phase-what-it-means-for-canada).

### Inferences
- **The UK CBAM is the lowest-effort expansion.** The same installation data, embedded-emissions calculations and supplier evidence serve EU and UK filings, with different thresholds, sectors (no electricity) and declarant (the UK importer). A "dual EU+UK CBAM evidence pack" is a clear product extension.
- **South Africa is a double-exposure market.** SA exporters pay domestic carbon tax and face EU/UK CBAM, so reconciling carbon-tax returns (SARS) with CBAM "carbon price paid" deduction evidence is a concrete, document-heavy agent task.
- Japan, China, India and Türkiye are mostly competitor-country context, and possibly an emerging-market expansion route (Türkiye and India SMEs exporting steel and aluminium to the EU face the same CBAM data asks). They matter less for Africa-focused GTM.

### Gaps
- Exact UK legislative citation not verified. HMRC registration-service opening date not confirmed.
- IISD 2026 BCA report contents (country tally) not read.
- South Africa basic-allowance conflict unresolved.

## 7. US/global disclosure: California SB 253/261, ISSB adoption in Africa (Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa/JSE, Morocco), SBTi, CDP

### Takeaway
California **SB 253** first Scope 1–2 reports are due **10 Nov 2026** (moved from 10 Aug), with no assurance in year one and Scope 3 from 2027. **SB 261** remains **enjoined by the Ninth Circuit** (since 18 Nov 2025). In Africa, ISSB is being adopted with phased mandates: **Kenya** mandatory for PIEs from 1 Jan 2027, large non-PIEs 2028 and SMEs 2029; **Nigeria** voluntary since Jan 2025 with conflicting mandatory dates of 2027 or 2028; **Ghana** listed by IFRS as adopting; **South Africa/JSE** guidance-based with no confirmed mandate. **SBTi Net-Zero Standard V2** was published 11 Jun 2026. **CDP 2026** widened in scope. Domestic ISSB mandates, with Scope 3 as a "near-universal gap", create a home-market data need for the same African manufacturers.

### Cited Findings
- **SB 253.** CARB approved the initial rule on 26 Feb 2026 with a 10 Aug 2026 deadline. On 24 Jun 2026 CARB deferred it to **10 Nov 2026**, and modified regulations (27 Jul 2026) confirmed this. There is no assurance for the first submission, and Scope 3 starts in 2027. The rule may be subject to OAL approval — [Sidley](https://environmentalhealthsafetybrief.sidley.com/2026/06/30/sb-253-update-carb-delays-reporting-deadline-to-november-2026-and-proposes-to-clarify-requirements/); [Mayer Brown, Aug 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/california-climate-disclosure-laws-carb-finalizes-its-initial-rulemaking-resets-the-2026-deadline-and-previews-the-2027-framework); [Simpson Thacher](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2026/07/22/carb-provides-new-guidance-on-sb-253-reporting); [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/March/California-Air-Resources-Board-Approves-Initial-Regulations-SB-253-SB-261).
- **SB 261.** The Ninth Circuit granted an injunction pending appeal on 18 Nov 2025 and heard oral argument on 9 Jan 2026. I found no merits ruling. SB 253 was not enjoined — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-ninth-circuit-enjoins-enforcement-of-california-climate-disclosure-law-sb-261); [Cooley, Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-26-californias-sb-253-and-sb-261-developments-and-litigation); [Harvard CorpGov](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2025/12/03/california-climate-disclosure-law-sb-261-implementation-halted-ninth-circuit-grants-injunction-pending-appeal/).
- **ISSB, global.** The IFRS Feb 2026 SCC update counts 39 jurisdictions and lists in EMEA: "EU, Ghana, Jordan, Kenya, Nigeria, Qatar, Rwanda, Switzerland, Tanzania, Türkiye, Uganda, UK, Zambia, Zimbabwe" — [IFRS SCC Feb 2026 agenda paper](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2026/february/scc/ap1-issb-updates.pdf).
- **Kenya (ICPAK).** Voluntary from 1 Jan 2024. Mandatory for PIEs from 1 Jan 2027, large non-PIEs from 1 Jan 2028, and SME non-PIEs from 1 Jan 2029 — [BDO East Africa](https://www.bdo-ea.com/en-gb/insights/the-future-of-sustainability-reporting-in-africa-key-insights-on-issb-and-ifrs-sustainability-discl); [ESG Source tracker](https://esgsource.com/page/issb-adoption-tracker-2026). (Secondary; ICPAK primary not fetched.)
- **Nigeria (FRC).** Voluntary from Jan 2025. **Conflicting** mandatory dates: Jan 2027 (per Anthesis, cited in an SSRN paper) vs 2028, with roadmap amendments under consultation in early 2026 — [SSRN (Faloore, 2026)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6987918); [The Eco Ledger](https://theecoledger.com/insights/ifrs-s1-and-ifrs-s2-explained-the-issb-standards-guide-for-2026).
- **South Africa / JSE.** The JSE Sustainability Disclosure Guidance is aligned with TCFD and ISSB, with no formal mandatory timeline (sources conflict) — [Grant Thornton SA](https://www.grantthornton.co.za/insights2/ifrs-s1--s2-a-new-era-for-sustainability-reporting-in-africa/); [Socious tracker](https://socious.io/blog/issb-adoption-tracker/).
- **Readiness.** A 2026 study of listed firms in Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya, Ghana and Rwanda finds readiness beyond the pioneers is narrow and Scope 3 measurement is "a near-universal gap" — [SSRN (Faloore, 2026)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6987918); [ResearchGate review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/406068344_ISSB_Standards_and_the_African_Preparedness_Gap_A_Critical_Review_of_Sustainability_Disclosure_Readiness_in_Sub-Saharan_Africa).
- **SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0.** Published 11 Jun 2026, it shifts from target-setting to an implementation framework and replaces the SME route with company categories A and B. Companies can use V2 from 31 Jan 2027 and choose V1 or V2 until Q1 2028 (WSP), though another source says V2 is mandatory from 2028 — [SBTi CNZS V2 PDF](https://files.sciencebasedtargets.org/production/files/Corporate-Net-Zero-Standard-version-2.pdf); [WSP briefing](https://www.wsp.com/-/media/insights/uk/documents/sbti-version-2-changes-briefing-2026.pdf); [Tanso](https://www.tanso.de/en/news/sbti-publishes-final-corporate-net-zero-standardv20).
- **CDP 2026.** Scope widened to ocean, plastics, scored forest commodities and water. The Supply Chain programme lets members request supplier disclosure, with a 2026 scoring deadline of 16 Sep — [Sunhat](https://www.getsunhat.com/blog/cdp-updates); [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/what-you-need-know-complete-cdp-supply-chain-questionnaire/).

### Inferences
- SB 253 creates Scope 3 data demand on African suppliers to large US companies from 2027, but this is weaker and less Africa-specific than EU-driven demand.
- **Kenya's 2027–2029 phased ISSB mandate, reaching SMEs in 2029, is the clearest domestic ISSB driver in Africa.** It creates home-market demand for the same emissions data an exporter already gathers for CBAM, EUDR or VS. A product that turns one evidence store into CBAM, VS, ISSB S2 and CDP outputs addresses the "same data, many formats" pain.
- Morocco: I found no AMMC ISSB adoption decision (see Gaps). Morocco is a high-priority CBAM, EUDR-adjacent and textile-ESPR market, so this is worth verifying.

### Gaps
- Ninth Circuit merits decision on SB 261. None found as of Jan 2026 sources, so it may have issued since.
- Morocco (AMMC), Ghana (ICAG/SEC timeline), Egypt (FRA) and Mauritius ISSB specifics not found.
- No CDP data on emerging-market supplier response rates or support.

## 8. Synthesis: which regimes hit African and emerging-market exporters hardest and are least served by tools?

### Takeaway
On exposure, deadline proximity and agent fit, the ranking for an Africa-focused, CBAM-adjacent SaaS is:
1. **EUDR** (30 Dec 2026; cocoa, coffee, rubber, timber, palm; origin-side data factory).
2. **UK CBAM** (1 Jan 2027; near-zero marginal build).
3. **EU Forced Labour Regulation** (14 Dec 2027; shares data with EUDR).
4. **VS-capped CSRD supplier requests plus domestic ISSB** (FY2027+; "one supplier passport").
5. **PPWR** (live since 12 Aug 2026) and **Battery Regulation due diligence** (Aug 2027; critical minerals).

The Methane Regulation, ETS2, maritime and aviation are low-fit (concentrated buyers, incumbents, or likely further delays).

### Cited Findings
- The barrier for African exporters is "not a tariff... but a missing dataset at the farm, factory, warehouse or port" — [Africa Sustainability Matters](https://africasustainabilitymatters.com/eus-carbon-border-tax-reforms-set-to-redraw-africa-europe-trade-dynamics-ahead-of-2026-rollout/).

### Inferences
- **Regime fit matrix** (inference, for the report writer):

| Regime | Africa exposure | Deadline | Agent-shaped tasks | Tool-market gap (origin side) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EUDR | Very high (cocoa CI/GH/NG/CM; coffee UG/ET/KE; rubber CI/LR; timber; palm) | 30 Dec 2026 | Messy coop registers to plots; geometry QA; batch-to-plot mass balance; legality docs; DDS packs | Large: buyer-side saturated, exporter/coop middle layer thin |
| UK CBAM | Medium (SA, EG, MZ steel/alu/fertiliser) | 1 Jan 2027 | Reuse of EU CBAM engine; UK importer packs | Large for exporters; few tools are Africa-focused |
| Forced Labour Reg. | High (cocoa, cotton, minerals, fishing) | 14 Dec 2027 | Labour-risk dossiers, audit ingestion, supplier mapping, response to authority inquiries | Large: market nascent |
| CSRD VS cap + CSDDD flow-down | Medium-high (all EU-facing suppliers) | FY2027 / 2029 | Pre-filled VS "supplier passport"; questionnaire auto-answer; cap-based pushback | Medium: VSME tools exist but EU-SME focused |
| Domestic ISSB (KE, NG, GH) | Medium (listed and large firms first, KE SMEs 2029) | 2027–2029 | S2 climate disclosure drafting from the same evidence store; Scope 3 | Medium-large |
| PPWR | Medium (packaged food/bev/cosmetics) | Live 12 Aug 2026 | Supplier material declarations, DoC packs | Medium |
| Battery Reg. DD/passport | High for minerals upstream (DRC, ZM, ZW) | Feb/Aug 2027 | OECD DD evidence, chain of custody, PCF later | Medium: incumbents (Circularise etc.) EU-OEM focused |
| ESPR DPP (steel, textiles) | Medium (N. Africa and East Africa apparel, SA steel) | ~2028+ compliance | BOM/material data, supplier declarations | Too early |
| Methane Reg. | High value, few firms (DZ, NG, AO, EG, MZ) | 2027, likely delayed | MRV evidence mapping, contract clauses, cargo attribution | Low fit for SME SaaS |
| ETS2 / maritime / aviation | Low | 2026–2028 | Operator MRV | Incumbent-served |
| SA carbon tax + CBAM | Medium (SA industrials) | Ongoing (Phase 2 2026) | Reconcile carbon-tax returns with CBAM deduction evidence | Medium |

- **Strategic pattern.** The regimes converge on one supplier-side data asset (installation/plot geodata, energy and fuel, materials, labour due diligence, certificates) that EU, UK and domestic regimes each request in different formats. The defensible agentic product is an **origin-side "compliance evidence graph" with regime-specific output generators** (CBAM, UK CBAM, EUDR DDS inputs, FLR dossier, VS, ISSB S2), rather than a separate point tool per regulation.
- **Least served, highest pain:** EUDR origin-side data (cooperatives and exporters in Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Cameroon, Nigeria, Uganda, Ethiopia), followed by FLR labour evidence for the same supply chains. Both are agent-shaped: unstructured documents, field data, supplier chasing in local languages over WhatsApp or SMS, classification, and evidence packs. This ranking is inference from the cited exposure and vendor-landscape findings, not from a measured market study.

### Gaps
- I found no single 2026 study quantifying the combined cost of EUDR, CBAM, FLR and CSDDD for African exporters.
- No quantitative market sizing (TAM/SAM) for origin-side compliance tooling in Africa.
- No primary-source verification of the pricing or customer counts for most EUDR vendors (vendor self-reports).
