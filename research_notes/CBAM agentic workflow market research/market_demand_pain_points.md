# CBAM Market Demand and Pain Points: EU Importers/Declarants and Non-EU Producers (state as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 12 Oct 2026. Evidence quality legend used below: [OFFICIAL] = EU institution / government; [LAW/BIG4] = law firm or Big-4 summary of legal text; [SURVEY] = survey with stated method; [ASSOC] = industry association position (advocacy); [VENDOR] = compliance-software / consultancy blog (commercial interest, treat as indicative only); [NEWS] = trade or general press. Findings from the 2023-2025 transitional period are flagged [TRANSITIONAL-ERA].

## 1. Current CBAM regulatory state in 2026 (definitive period, Omnibus, dates, verification, defaults, Dec 2025 package)

### Takeaway
The definitive regime has applied since 1 Jan 2026, but the cash/declaration cycle is back-loaded: certificate sales open 1 Feb 2027 and the first annual declaration plus surrender for 2026 imports is due 30 Sep 2027. The Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2025/2083, in force 20 Oct 2025) removed about 182,000 small importers (~90%) via a 50-tonne annual threshold while keeping more than 99% of emissions. That makes the remaining declarant market smaller and more concentrated, and puts the bottleneck on getting verified actual data from non-EU installations.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis and timing**
- CBAM "applies in its definitive regime from 1 January 2026"; transitional phase ran 2023-2025; covers cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen [OFFICIAL] — [European Commission CBAM page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en)
- The Omnibus simplification was officially published by the Commission on 20 Oct 2025 [OFFICIAL] — [Commission news, 20 Oct 2025](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/officially-published-simplifications-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-2025-10-20_en). PwC gives adoption on 8 Oct 2025 and publication 17 Oct 2025, with entry into force 20 Oct 2025. Other sources give publication as 20 Oct. The exact OJ date is minor, but sources conflict [LAW/BIG4] — [PwC Malta](https://www.pwc.com/mt/en/publications/tax-legal/regulation-eu-2025-2083-simplifying-the-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam.html); [EY Tax News 22 Oct 2025](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-2136); [Decerna](https://www.decerna.co.uk/eu-publishes-long-awaited-amending-regulation-to-simplify-cbam/)
- Parliament approved the deal 617-18 (other outlets report 564-20, which was the earlier negotiating-mandate vote) [NEWS] — [Seneca ESG / ESG Today](https://senecaesg.com/insights/eu-lawmakers-agree-to-exempt-90-of-companies-from-cbam-import-carbon-tax); [Euronews, 11 Sep 2025](https://euronews.com/my-europe/2025/09/11/small-businesses-exempt-from-eus-tax-at-the-border)

**50-tonne de minimis threshold**
- Replaces the old EUR 150-per-consignment exemption with a single threshold of 50 t net mass per importer per calendar year. It is cumulative across iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers and cement, and excludes electricity and hydrogen. If the threshold is exceeded, all CBAM goods imported that year are covered. The registry flags importers at 90% of the threshold, splitting imports is prohibited, and the Commission reviews the threshold annually (it can change it by delegated act) to keep at least 99% of emissions in scope [LAW/BIG4] — [Mayer Brown, 17 Oct 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead); [EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-2136); [EP Legislative Observatory](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1853717)
- The Commission projects about 182,000 importers exempted, "mostly SMEs and individuals", while covering more than 99% of emissions. The original Feb 2025 proposal framed this as ~90% of importers [OFFICIAL/LAW] — [Commission news](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/officially-published-simplifications-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-2025-10-20_en); [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102k2h4/eu-commission-proposes-changes-to-carbon-boarder-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-as); [A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/insights-publications/omnibus-package-amendments-proposed-to-the-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism)
- DIHK (June 2026) restates that the threshold excludes about 90% of importers. It also says SMEs without compliance teams or IT resources still struggle despite it [ASSOC] — [DIHK Impuls, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.dihk.de/de/newsroom/dihk-impuls-cbam-anpassungen-dringend-noetig-182882)
- Edge case: sources differ on whether exactly 50.0 t is exempt ("up to 50 t" vs "exceed 50 t"). Check the Annex text [LAW] — [Eurometal](https://eurometal.net/eu-adopts-cbam-amendment-introducing-50-mt-exemption-and-simplified-reporting/); [Crowe](https://www.crowe.com/ua/croweacu/en-us/news/the-cbam-simplification-regulation)

**Authorised declarant status**
- Importers expecting to exceed the threshold must apply before exceeding it. Importers and indirect customs representatives who applied by 31 Mar 2026 may keep importing provisionally until a decision is made [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead)
- Above 50 t, imports may only be carried out by importers holding a CBAM authorisation [OFFICIAL, national CA] — [Luxembourg Environment Administration, "Who must declare"](https://environnement.public.lu/fr/emweltprozeduren/Autorisations/cbam-en/who-must-declare.html)
- One vendor claims about 12,000 entities had applied for authorised declarant status by early 2026. I found no official source for this, so treat it as unverified [VENDOR] — [cbamguide.com verification](https://cbamguide.com/importers/verification/) / [SMM/metal.com](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104031152-smm-analysis-cbam-costs-are-taking-effect-verification-capacity-lags-what-stainless-steel-exporters-need-to-know)

**Certificates, declaration, holding, penalties**
- Certificate sales start 1 Feb 2027 on a central platform and cover 2026 imports. 2026-vintage certificates are priced at the quarterly average of 2026 ETS prices, then at the weekly average of auction prices. Repurchase requests are due by 31 Oct, and unused certificates are cancelled on 1 Nov (first cancellation 1 Nov 2027) [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead)
- The annual declaration and surrender is due 30 Sep of the following year, so the first is due 30 Sep 2027 for 2026 imports (moved from 31 May). The quarterly holding requirement drops from 80% to 50% of embedded emissions imported year to date, adjusted for free allocation [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead); [Andersen Italy](https://it.andersen.com/en/cbam-2026-obligations-deadlines-and-impacts-for-importers/). Some vendor pages still cite 31 May 2027. That is outdated [VENDOR] — [CarbonChain](https://www.carbonchain.com/cbam)
- CBAM obligation phase-in: certificates cover 2.5% of embedded emissions in 2026 (CBAM factor 97.5% free allocation), rising to 100% by 2034 [LAW] — [Werksmans / Polity, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.polity.org.za/article/south-african-exporters-take-note-2026-08-21). Illustration: Egyptian urea at 2.3-2.6 tCO2e/t has a gross cost of €172-195/t at €75, a net cost below €5/t in 2026 and about €84-95/t by 2030 (CBAM factor 48.5%) [VENDOR] — [cbamguide Egypt](https://cbamguide.com/countries/egypt/)
- Penalties are €100/tCO2e (aligned with the ETS), with reductions for minor errors. Importing above the threshold without authorisation carries 3-5x the penalty [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead)
- One vendor cites a published Q2 2026 CBAM price of €75.28/tCO2e. I could not confirm it against the Commission price page (the cmtradelaw page on the first price release returned 403) [VENDOR] — [cbamguide verification](https://cbamguide.com/importers/verification/)

**Verification**
- Verification by an accredited verifier is required only when actual values are used, not for defaults. Verifiers may request registration within two months of accreditation, but not before 1 Sep 2026 [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead)
- The Commission published CBAM verification and accreditation guidance (for verifiers and National Accreditation Bodies) on 24 Aug 2026 [LAW] — [Werksmans / Polity](https://www.polity.org.za/article/south-african-exporters-take-note-2026-08-21)
- Vendor claims: remote-only verification is not allowed in the first cycle, and there are about 403 EU-ETS-accredited verifiers. Both are unverified [VENDOR] — [SMM/metal.com](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104031152-smm-analysis-cbam-costs-are-taking-effect-verification-capacity-lags-what-stainless-steel-exporters-need-to-know); [cbamguide](https://cbamguide.com/compliance/verification/)

**Default values (Dec 2025 implementing act)**
- The Omnibus says defaults are based on the average emission intensity of the 10 highest-emitting exporting countries where reliable data is lacking [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead)
- The Commission published provisional implementing acts and annexes in Dec 2025 [BIG4] — [EY, 23 Dec 2025](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-2607-european-commission-publishes-provisional-implementing-acts-and-their-annexes-regarding-cbam). Secondary sources cite Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621 for defaults. The mark-up is 10% (2026), 20% (2027) and 30% (2028+) for steel, aluminium and cement, and a flat 1% for fertilisers, to be re-evaluated by end-2027 [OFFICIAL, national CA for fertiliser 1%] — [Luxembourg CA certificates page](https://environnement.public.lu/fr/emweltprozeduren/Autorisations/cbam-en/cbam-certificated-and-price.html); [VENDOR] [greensutra fertiliser](https://greensutra.in/question/cbam-fertiliser-flat-markup/). One source cites 2025/2547 instead. Check the OJ number [VENDOR] — [carbmee](https://www.carbmee.com/knowledge-insights/blog-article/cbam-primary-data-vs-default-values)
- Illustrative defaults reproduced from Annex I for India: ammonia 3.28, urea 2.17, nitric acid 2.01, DAP 0.81 tCO2e/t before mark-up [VENDOR] — [greensutra](https://greensutra.in/services/cbam-fertiliser/)
- DIHK: the Dec 2025 defaults (especially for China and Indonesia) are "well above" earlier reference values. It calls the annual mark-ups disproportionate and asks for 3 years' lead time and a public complaint portal [ASSOC] — [DIHK Impuls, Jun 2026](https://www.dihk.de/de/newsroom/dihk-impuls-cbam-anpassungen-dringend-noetig-182882)
- Conflict: some vendors say that after 2025 the choice of defaults vs actuals is free. Older guidance required justification [VENDOR] — [carbmee](https://www.carbmee.com/knowledge-insights/blog-article/cbam-primary-data-vs-default-values)

**Third-country carbon price deduction**
- Declarants may deduct carbon prices effectively paid abroad (including in a third country other than the origin). From 2027 the Commission may publish default carbon prices per country [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead). Egypt had no carbon price as of Apr 2026, so no deduction applies [VENDOR] — [cbamguide Egypt](https://cbamguide.com/countries/egypt/). The South African carbon tax is the main lever, but only with verified data [LAW] — [Lex Africa, Sep 2026](https://lexafrica.com/2026/09/south-african-exporters-face-rising-costs-under-eu-carbon-rules/)

**Dec 2025 package: downstream extension, anti-circumvention, TDF**
- On 17 Dec 2025 the Commission proposed (COM(2025) 0419) adding about 180 steel- and aluminium-intensive downstream products from 1 Jan 2028. It also proposed anti-circumvention rules (traceability reporting, pre-consumer scrap counted in calculations, electricity rules) and a Temporary Decarbonisation Fund: 25% of CBAM revenues in 2028-29, covering 2026-27 production, tied to decarbonisation investment [LAW/ICAP] — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/trade-compliance-resource-hub/4874/commission-proposes-expansion-of-eu-cbam-to-downstream-goods-and-addresses-potential-cbam-abusive-practices/); [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-cbam-enters-compliance-phase-and-outlines-path-ahead); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/cbam-expansion-poised-to-impact-global-supply-chains)
- The Council adopted its general approach on 12 Jun 2026, with about 200 metal-intensive goods (industrial, construction, electrical equipment). EP ENVI extended the TDF to fertiliser producers and downstream users (urea, AN, AS) and to 2027-2029. No final text had been found as of Oct 2026 [NEWS] — [OneStopESG](https://onestopesg.com/esg-news/eu-cbam-carbon-border-tax-expansion)
- DIHK calls expansion from 2028 premature [ASSOC] — [DIHK](https://www.dihk.de/de/newsroom/dihk-impuls-cbam-anpassungen-dringend-noetig-182882)

### Inferences
- The first hard "pay" moment is Feb-Sep 2027. Demand for producer-side verified data peaks across Q4 2026 to Q2 2027, because actuals for 2026 imports need verification before 30 Sep 2027 and verifier registration only opened from 1 Sep 2026.
- Because verification is required only for actuals, the commercial decision for each importer-supplier pair is whether the verification cost is lower than the default-value premium. A tool that quantifies this per installation and product is directly decision-useful.
- Fertilisers' 1% mark-up (vs 10-30%) weakens the default-penalty argument for Morocco/Egypt fertiliser in 2026-27. The value proposition there rests more on actuals being lower than country defaults and on the carbon price deduction than on avoiding mark-ups.
- Downstream extension from 2028 (180-200 products) would sharply increase the number of affected importers and producers. That is a sizeable second wave, but not yet law.

### Gaps
- No official count of authorised declarants granted or applied for in 2026 (the 12,000 figure is vendor-sourced).
- Did not retrieve the Commission's official CBAM certificate price publications for 2026.
- Did not confirm the final OJ number/date of the default-value implementing act, or the exact list of Dec 2025 implementing/delegated acts (benchmarks, verification, accreditation).
- Status of trilogue on downstream/anti-circumvention after Jun 2026 unknown.

## 2. Pain points reported by EU importers/declarants

### Takeaway
Survey evidence (mostly German chamber surveys, 2024, transitional-era) consistently ranks obtaining data from third-country suppliers as the #1 problem (~91% in IHK NRW). Next come registry/portal usability (~70% rated it unsatisfactory in IHK Hessen) and effort out of proportion to volumes. In 2026 the complaints shifted to verifier scarcity, high Dec-2025 defaults, legal uncertainty and unclear authority responsibilities. Hard quantitative 2026 surveys are scarce. Most current "evidence" is association advocacy or vendor content.

### Cited Findings
- [SURVEY, TRANSITIONAL-ERA] IHK NRW survey (12-31 Aug 2024; 404 responses on the challenges question): highest agreement (~91%) for "information gathering from third-country suppliers". Other listed options were the reporting portal, effort too high for product volumes, report scope, and lack of capacity. The mapping of the other percentages could not be confirmed from the PDF text, and the PDF now returns 404 — [IHK NRW results PDF (via search snippet)](https://www.ihk-nrw.de/blueprint/servlet/resource/blob/6265662/b62ce0024a4ab5f99610ffad4041051a/cbam-ergebnisse-umfrage-data.pdf)
- [SURVEY, TRANSITIONAL-ERA] IHK Frankfurt/Hessen 2024 survey (n=99 companies): only 15 spent more than 25 hours per quarter on CBAM reporting. The IHK believes this is because most suppliers had no data yet. Almost 70% rated the reporting portal unsatisfactory to very bad. Anecdote: none of one firm's suppliers had ever heard of CBAM — [IHK Frankfurt CBAM Umfrage 2024](https://www.frankfurt-main.ihk.de/international/beratung-kontakte/esg-compliance/cbam-umfrage-2024-6320252)
- [ASSOC, TRANSITIONAL-ERA] DIHK/BDI May 2024: effort for the first report was sometimes "out of all proportion" to the reported emissions (the effort list is in the annex). It asked for an online self-assessment tool by third country and HS code showing which supplier data to request — [DIHK/BDI CBAM paper](https://www.dihk.de/resource/blob/140494/a93b7a9e8812a3a1e143a119ed153a9d/cbam-papier-dihk-und-bdi-data.pdf)
- [ASSOC, 2026] DIHK Impuls (29 Jun 2026) on definitive-period problems:
  - about 40,000 production installations need verification, with not enough accredited verifiers, so firms will be pushed onto defaults
  - Dec 2025 defaults are well above earlier references
  - rules arrive at short notice and open questions remain (inward processing, returned goods, reusable packaging)
  - responsibilities between customs, DEHSt and the EU portal are unclear
  - no export relief

  Demands: extend verification deadlines, drop the mark-ups, and provide a multilingual cost calculator and an EU self-assessment tool linked to Access2Markets — [DIHK](https://www.dihk.de/de/newsroom/dihk-impuls-cbam-anpassungen-dringend-noetig-182882)
- [SURVEY, qualitative, undated; no sample size disclosed] FACE (aluminium consumers) survey: pain points are reporting/compliance, calculating embedded emissions, regulatory complexity, relationships with non-EU suppliers, and the risk of errors and penalties. Importers "repeatedly request and verify the same emissions data across the supply chain". One respondent proposed a public, certified EU registry of carbon values by installation and HS code. Smaller firms are more uncertain and lack expertise, data and compliance resources. A "clear majority" support the downstream extension — [FACE survey results](https://face-aluminium.com/face-survey-results-impact-of-cbam-tariffs-and-supply-risks-on-the-aluminium-value-chain/)
- [VENDOR, TRANSITIONAL-ERA, Jul 2024] IntegrityNext: importers depend on suppliers' "voluntary support", and data quality is insufficient. No quantitative data — [IntegrityNext](https://www.integritynext.com/resources/blog/article/insights-from-the-first-10-months-of-eu-cbam)
- [VENDOR] Recurring claims: suppliers lack systems, expertise or incentives. Existing ESG/LCA/PCF figures cannot be reused and must be recalculated at installation level with the EU methodology. Importers manually correct incomplete data — [Zevero](https://www.zevero.earth/blog/cbam-supplier-emissions-data); [CarbonChain](https://www.carbonchain.com/cbam); [AmSpec](https://www.amspecgroup.com/resources/cbam-compliance-in-2026-a-practical-readiness-guide-for-producers-traders-and-importers/)
- [VENDOR] Default vs actual gap claims vary widely, from "3-4x higher" for steel to "20-40% higher". These are not reliable as general figures — [carbmee](https://www.carbmee.com/knowledge-insights/blog-article/cbam-primary-data-vs-default-values)

### Inferences
- The importer pain is fundamentally a supplier-data acquisition and quality problem. A producer-side tool that outputs data in the format the importer needs (the CBAM communication template / XML-compatible installation data) directly addresses the #1 importer complaint, so the producer-side and importer-side value propositions align.
- The FACE "same data requested repeatedly" point suggests demand for a share-once, many-recipients model: a producer publishes verified installation data to multiple EU customers.
- Post-Omnibus, the remaining ~10% of importers (~20,000 if the ~182,000 = 90% figure holds) are larger and more likely to have internal teams or Big-4 or enterprise tools. "SME importer" demand is now mostly mid-caps just over 50 t, plus customs representatives.

### Gaps
- No 2026 quantitative survey found of supplier response rates (e.g., "% of suppliers that provided actual data"). The Commission's transitional-period statistics on default-value usage were not found.
- EUROCHAMBRES, BusinessEurope, Eurofer and Fertilizers Europe 2026 surveys not located in this pass (they may exist; only position statements were seen indirectly).
- No Big-4 (PwC/Deloitte/EY/KPMG) CBAM readiness survey with sample sizes was found in this pass.

## 3. Pain points reported by non-EU producers (Africa, India, Turkey, Vietnam)

### Takeaway
Producer-side pain centres on MRV capacity (installation-level monitoring, especially of indirect/electricity emissions), scarce and expensive accredited verification (often requiring international verifiers), default-value exposure, and price pressure from EU buyers. African exposure is concentrated: Mozambique aluminium (Mozal, now on care and maintenance since 15 Mar 2026), South African steel and aluminium, and Egyptian/Moroccan fertiliser. Readiness is uneven: large aluminium producers are relatively advanced, steel and SMEs much less so.

### Cited Findings
**Africa: exposure modelling (mostly pre-definitive, 2023-era design)**
- ACF/LSE study: CBAM could cut African exports to the EU of aluminium by up to 13.9%, iron and steel 8.2%, fertiliser 3.9% and cement 3.1%, with some diversion to China and India. The GDP effect on any African LDC is expected to be at most 0.17%. The $27bn loss figure refers to an all-sectors extended scenario [TRANSITIONAL-ERA modelling] — [ACF & LSE report](https://www.lse.ac.uk/africa/assets/Documents/AFC-and-LSE-Report-Implications-for-Africa-of-a-CBAM-in-the-EU.pdf); [Mining Weekly, May 2023](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/incoming-cbam-could-see-african-aluminium-iron-exports-plummet-2023-05-11)
- 14-47% of Mozambique's exports fall under CBAM coverage, primarily Mozal aluminium. An IMF-attributed estimate puts the GDP impact at 1.6%, which conflicts with LSE's at most 0.17% for LDCs [secondary] — [Energy Monitor](https://www.energymonitor.ai/carbon-markets/how-cbam-threatens-africas-sustainable-development/); [SAIIA](https://saiia.org.za/research/the-impact-of-the-cbam-on-african-economies-and-the-role-of-the-afcfta/)
- 2026 SOAS paper: African fertiliser exports (to the EU, per paper context) are USD 2,923m, dominated by Egypt (1,247m), Morocco (719m) and Algeria (684m) — [SOAS, Persenda, May 2026](https://www.soas.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-05/16%20Persenda%20CBAM.pdf)
- Sandbag (Oct 2025): imports from Mozambique, Ghana and Cameroon could become more profitable relative to others, while those from Egypt, Algeria and South Africa become less profitable. Aluminium could gain from resource shuffling (+€118m) — [Sandbag](https://sandbag.be/2025/10/14/cbam-africa-impact/)
- Egypt: about 46% of fertiliser exports go to the EU (OCI, ElSewedy cited) [VENDOR] — [cbamguide Egypt](https://cbamguide.com/countries/egypt/)

**Mozambique/Mozal (critical for carbonTraceAI's beachhead)**
- South32 placed Mozal on care and maintenance on 15 Mar 2026 after failing to secure affordable power beyond March 2026 (Eskom/HCB, drought). One-off costs are about US$60m, and the smelter had more than 2,500 staff. Ownership: South32 63.7%, IDC 32.4%, GoM 3.9% [NEWS / company SENS] — [Engineering News, 16 Mar 2026](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/south32-places-mozal-on-care-and-maintenance-2026-03-16); [Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/news/south32s-mozal-aluminium-care-and-maintenance/); [SENS](https://www.sharedata.co.za/sens.asp?id=541988)

**South Africa**
- TIPS (2024): SA is "not ready financially and administratively" to comply in the transition period [TRANSITIONAL-ERA] — [TIPS](https://www.tips.org.za/research-archive/sustainable-growth/green-economy-2/item/4791-tips-south-africa-iron-steel-and-aluminium-industries-readiness-to-respond-to-cbam)
- Earlier analyses found inconsistent plant-level data collection in aluminium and iron/steel, a lack of third-party verification, and insufficient Scope 2 records (Eskom grid) [TRANSITIONAL-ERA] — [ResearchGate, SA readiness](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385903577_South_Africa_Iron_Steel_and_Aluminium_Industries_Readiness_to_Respond_to_CBAM); [ResearchGate, SA export sector](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392924826_The_EU_Carbon_Border_Adjustment_Mechanism_CBAM_Implications_Risks_and_Strategic_Responses_for_South_Africa's_Export_Sector)
- 2026 aluminium policy brief: major producers (Hulamin, South32 Hillside) have emissions accounting systems. However, there are few accredited local verifiers, so firms depend on costly international services. It recommends national accreditation pathways and local verifier training — [AFPH aluminium policy brief](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/b2d05cc3-7b8c-4f0d-a62a-66af7434f28f/downloads/f7ea9290-c2f9-4e3c-82b2-d08b90c4c0a0/AFPH_Policy%20Brief%20Aluminium%20Sector-12AUG.pdf?ver=1786527675602)
- The SA government (dtic) officially rejects CBAM as unilateral and pushes for recognition of the SA carbon tax. No dtic verification support programme was confirmed — [OrePulse](https://www.orepulse.com/news/South-Africa-Confronts-EU-Carbon-Border-Mechanism); [Lex Africa, Sep 2026](https://lexafrica.com/2026/09/south-african-exporters-face-rising-costs-under-eu-carbon-rules/); [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/south-african-exporters-take-note/)

**Morocco**
- OCP targets 100% renewable electricity by 2027 and carbon neutrality by 2040, and is investing $7bn in green ammonia. The benefit depends on EU recognition of the reductions under CBAM rules, and financing costs rise without validated savings [NEWS] — [Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/127493-127493.html); [L'Economiste](https://weekly.leconomiste.com/editorial-formidable/)

**India**
- GTRI-linked reporting: Indian steel and aluminium exports to the EU fell 24.4% (US$7.71bn in FY24 to US$5.82bn in FY25), with iron and steel down 35.1%. Exporters may need 15-22% price cuts. This is a think tank with an advocacy angle, and causality is not established [NEWS] — [Basis Point Insight](https://www.basispointinsight.com/Story/-india-s-metal-exports-to-eu-plunge-24--as-cbam-reporting-hits-msmes-_30e1360663c9.html); [alcircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/2026-brings-in-cbam-a-challenge-for-european-importers-or-a-price-for-indian-exporters-116776)
- 25,000-30,000 Indian MSMEs at risk (secondary summary of Business Standard; untraceable to a primary source) — [Vision IAS, 29 Jan 2026](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-01-29/business-standard/economy/india-eu-free-trade-agreement-cbam-rules-strain-indias-steel-msmes)
- Case study: a small Indian fastener/steel exporter hired a consultant for about ₹2 lakh/yr (~€2,000) [NEWS, anecdote] — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/how-the-eus-carbon-border-tax-is-reshaping-indian-msmes)

**Cross-country verification bottleneck**
- About 40,000 installations need verification against insufficient verifier capacity (DIHK) — [DIHK](https://www.dihk.de/de/newsroom/dihk-impuls-cbam-anpassungen-dringend-noetig-182882). The site-visit requirement could push importers heavily onto defaults (expert quote) — [Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/more/renewables/the-nuts-and-bolts-of-cbam-935394)
- Liability sits with the EU importer, but the cost flows upstream via price pressure on exporters — [cbamguide Morocco](https://cbamguide.com/countries/morocco/); FACE respondents expect non-EU suppliers to absorb CBAM via price cuts — [FACE](https://face-aluminium.com/face-survey-results-impact-of-cbam-tariffs-and-supply-risks-on-the-aluminium-value-chain/)

### Inferences
- Mozal's shutdown removes the single largest African CBAM aluminium exporter, and one of carbonTraceAI's named beachhead targets, from the market at least temporarily. The Mozambique aluminium thesis needs re-validation, and South Africa (Hillside, Hulamin), Egypt/Morocco fertiliser, and steel/cement are more immediate targets.
- Large producers (South32 Hillside, OCP) likely have in-house or Big-4 MRV. The underserved producer segment is mid-sized and smaller steel/cement/fertiliser plants and secondary aluminium processors lacking MRV systems.
- Verifier scarcity in Africa suggests demand for "verification-ready" data packages (an audit trail from ERP to emissions) that cut verifier days and cost.

### Gaps
- No primary 2026 data on how many African installations have obtained verified CBAM data, or on verifier presence in Africa.
- No primary Turkey or Vietnam producer survey was found in this pass. Nigeria was not covered (low CBAM exposure).
- Afreximbank, UNCTAD, World Bank and ERCST 2026 studies were not retrieved; mostly 2023-25 era modelling was found.
- No direct OCP or Egyptian producer (OCI/Fertiglobe, Abu Qir) statements on 2026 verification status.

## 4. Cost data: consultants, verification, per-company compliance, staff hours

### Takeaway
Verification is the dominant hard cost: indicative €5k-50k per installation per year, depending on complexity (fertiliser complexes at the top). Consultant fees and per-company costs are only anecdotal (e.g., ₹2 lakh/yr consultant for an Indian SME; ₹15-20 lakh estimated MSME setup). The best staff-time data is transitional-era: most German importers spent 25 hours or less per quarter. Almost all cost figures come from vendors or press, not systematic studies.

### Cited Findings
- Verification €5,000-50,000 per installation: about €5k-12k for a simple single-product mill, €30k-50k for complex petrochemical/fertiliser plants. Drivers are complexity, number of products, size and travel for the mandatory site visit [VENDOR] — [cbamguide verification](https://cbamguide.com/importers/verification/); [cbamjournal](https://www.cbamjournal.com/post/eu-cbam-verification-2026-2027)
- Alternative band of €5,000-25,000 per installation per year [VENDOR/NEWS] — [SMM/metal.com](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104031152-smm-analysis-cbam-costs-are-taking-effect-verification-capacity-lags-what-stainless-steel-exporters-need-to-know)
- Importer view: 10 medium-complexity installations cost about €120k-250k in first-cycle verification, and 50+ installations can exceed €2m [VENDOR estimate] — [CBAM Tools](https://cbam-tools.com/blog/cbam-emissions-verification-guide-importers)
- Default penalty illustration for Indian steel: default 3.2 tCO2e/t, rising to 3.52 (2026, +10%) and 3.84 (2027, +20%). An estimated €137.60/t difference vs verified actuals at €80/t (illustrative) [VENDOR] — [cbamguide verification](https://cbamguide.com/importers/verification/)
- India MSME compliance cost estimate of ₹15-20 lakh (~€15k-20k), covering carbon accounting, measurement, verification and reporting [secondary] — [Vajiram & Ravi explainer](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/eu-carbon-border-adjustment/). A small exporter's consultant costs about ₹2 lakh/yr — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/how-the-eus-carbon-border-tax-is-reshaping-indian-msmes)
- Indian MSME process changes (e.g., solar) raise production costs by 5-8% [secondary] — [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-01-29/business-standard/economy/india-eu-free-trade-agreement-cbam-rules-strain-indias-steel-msmes)
- Importer staff time [SURVEY, TRANSITIONAL-ERA]: 15 of 99 Hessian firms spent more than 25 h/quarter — [IHK Frankfurt](https://www.frankfurt-main.ihk.de/international/beratung-kontakte/esg-compliance/cbam-umfrage-2024-6320252). The DIHK/BDI annex lists disproportionate first-report efforts (detail not extracted) — [DIHK/BDI](https://www.dihk.de/resource/blob/140494/a93b7a9e8812a3a1e143a119ed153a9d/cbam-papier-dihk-und-bdi-data.pdf)
- The central platform is funded by fees from authorised declarants (amount not found) — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead)
- Mozal care-and-maintenance one-off cost of about US$60m, a scale reference for producer-side economics, not CBAM-specific — [Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/south32-places-mozal-on-care-and-maintenance-2026-03-16)

### Inferences
- For a mid-size producer, the annual hard cost is roughly verification at €5k-50k plus consultant or internal MRV time. A SaaS priced at a fraction of that (low thousands of €/yr per installation) that measurably reduces verifier days is plausible. A price point near ₹2 lakh/yr (~€2k) is the anchor for small Indian or African SMEs, based on one anecdote.
- The default-vs-actual differential (around €100+/t of product in some steel cases) is the main ROI argument for steel/aluminium. For fertilisers (1% mark-up) ROI depends on actuals being below the country default.

### Gaps
- No Commission impact-assessment figure on per-company administrative cost for the definitive period was retrieved.
- No systematic consultant fee survey (EU or Africa) was found. Big-4 fee levels are unknown.
- No African-specific verification quotes were found.

## 5. Underserved segments: small producers, developing-country exporters, customs brokers/indirect representatives

### Takeaway
Three gaps stand out:
- Small and mid non-EU producers without MRV systems or local verifiers. Evidence: SA policy briefs, Indian MSME reporting, DIHK on verifier shortage.
- Mid-cap EU importers just above 50 t who lack compliance teams. Evidence: DIHK, FACE.
- Customs representatives, who can now act as declarants or delegated submitters but carry liability and resourcing burdens.

The Omnibus formalised delegation, which creates a B2B2B channel through brokers.

### Cited Findings
- Indirect customs representatives may apply for authorised declarant status (provisional import if applied by 31 Mar 2026). They are liable for penalties when acting as declarant, except where acting for an EU-established importer without agreeing to that role. Declarants can delegate submission to an EU-established third party with an EORI number but remain responsible [LAW] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead)
- National CA: where a customs representative does not offer CBAM declaration services, the obligation stays with the importer, who can appoint a third-party provider [OFFICIAL] — [Luxembourg CA](https://environnement.public.lu/fr/emweltprozeduren/Autorisations/cbam-en/who-must-declare.html); [Luxembourg FAQ](https://environnement.public.lu/fr/emweltprozeduren/Autorisations/cbam-en/faq.html)
- [ASSOC, TRANSITIONAL-ERA 2023] CLECAT: draft rules imposed an "onerous obligation" on forwarders/customs agents acting as indirect representatives, with timelines too short. In 2025 CLECAT welcomed delegation and the 50-t threshold. No 2026 CLECAT statement was found — [CLECAT feedback 2023 (via FENEX)](https://www.fenex.nl/app/uploads/2023/08/Feedback-CLECAT-CBAM-implementing-regulation.pdf)
- PwC Belgium: most importers use (often several) customs representatives, and direct representatives were prohibited from being the CBAM reporting declarant in the transitional period, which created a gap [BIG4, TRANSITIONAL-ERA] — [PwC Belgium](https://news.pwc.be/?p=14786)
- Freight forwarders are now publishing CBAM compliance calendars for 2026 [VENDOR] — [GoFreight](https://gofreight.com/blog/freight-forwarder-compliance-calendar)
- SMEs without compliance or IT resources struggle even with the 50-t threshold [ASSOC] — [DIHK](https://www.dihk.de/de/newsroom/dihk-impuls-cbam-anpassungen-dringend-noetig-182882). Smaller aluminium-consuming firms show more uncertainty and capability gaps — [FACE](https://face-aluminium.com/face-survey-results-impact-of-cbam-tariffs-and-supply-risks-on-the-aluminium-value-chain/)
- Developing-country producers face costly international verifiers and need local accreditation and training — [AFPH brief](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/b2d05cc3-7b8c-4f0d-a62a-66af7434f28f/downloads/f7ea9290-c2f9-4e3c-82b2-d08b90c4c0a0/AFPH_Policy%20Brief%20Aluminium%20Sector-12AUG.pdf?ver=1786527675602). Older recommendations call for SARS/DTIC/DFFE-facilitated MRV training — [ResearchGate SA](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392924826_The_EU_Carbon_Border_Adjustment_Mechanism_CBAM_Implications_Risks_and_Strategic_Responses_for_South_Africa's_Export_Sector)
- A market for country-specific CBAM software is visibly emerging (e.g., "best CBAM software in Egypt" listicles), suggesting local competition [VENDOR] — [The Sustainability Cloud](https://www.thesustainabilitycloud.com/?p=30054)

### Inferences
- Brokers and forwarders aggregate many sub-50-t and over-50-t importers, so they are a potential distribution channel. A product that lets a broker handle multiple client declarations and pull producer data could be leveraged.
- Africa-focused positioning is differentiated against EU/US/India-centric vendors, but the addressable African producer pool is small and concentrated (a handful of smelters, fertiliser majors, steel/cement plants). Mozal's closure shrinks it further.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on the share of CBAM declarations filed by customs representatives vs importers in 2026.
- No 2026 CLECAT/FIATA position found.

## 6. What customers explicitly ask for (features, price points, language, offline/Excel)

### Takeaway
The explicit asks found are from associations and survey respondents, not product-specific requests:
- self-assessment tools by country/HS code
- multilingual cost calculators
- a shared or public registry of installation-level carbon values, so producers don't answer the same request repeatedly
- clear authority responsibilities and longer lead times

Evidence on price points and on Excel/offline workflow preferences is thin and anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- DIHK (Jun 2026) asks for a multilingual cost calculator, an EU online self-assessment tool linked to Access2Markets, a public complaint portal on default values, and extended verification deadlines [ASSOC] — [DIHK](https://www.dihk.de/de/newsroom/dihk-impuls-cbam-anpassungen-dringend-noetig-182882)
- DIHK/BDI (2024) asked for a self-assessment tool that shows, by third country and HS code, which data to request from suppliers [ASSOC, TRANSITIONAL-ERA] — [DIHK/BDI](https://www.dihk.de/resource/blob/140494/a93b7a9e8812a3a1e143a119ed153a9d/cbam-papier-dihk-und-bdi-data.pdf)
- FACE respondents asked for an end to repeated requests and verification of the same data, and for a public, certified EU registry of carbon values by installation and HS code [SURVEY, qualitative] — [FACE](https://face-aluminium.com/face-survey-results-impact-of-cbam-tariffs-and-supply-risks-on-the-aluminium-value-chain/)
- Registry/portal UX: almost 70% of Hessian firms rated the portal unsatisfactory to very bad [SURVEY, TRANSITIONAL-ERA] — [IHK Frankfurt](https://www.frankfurt-main.ihk.de/international/beratung-kontakte/esg-compliance/cbam-umfrage-2024-6320252)
- Price anchor: a small Indian exporter pays about ₹2 lakh/yr for a CBAM consultant [anecdote] — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/how-the-eus-carbon-border-tax-is-reshaping-indian-msmes)
- Importers need installation-level data recalculated under the EU methodology; generic ESG/LCA/PCF data is not accepted [VENDOR] — [AmSpec](https://www.amspecgroup.com/resources/cbam-compliance-in-2026-a-practical-readiness-guide-for-producers-traders-and-importers/); [carbonsettle (Indian exporter guide)](https://carbonsettle.com/blog/what-your-eu-importer-actually-does-with-the-cbam-data-you-send)

### Inferences
- Features the evidence implies:
  - producer-to-many-importer data sharing (one verified dataset, many recipients)
  - a default-vs-actual cost calculator per product, country and year (mark-up aware, with the 1% fertiliser exception)
  - HS-code/country self-assessment
  - verifier-ready audit trail and evidence pack
  - multilingual UI (DIHK asks for multilingual; for Africa this likely means French for Morocco/Francophone, Arabic for Egypt, Portuguese for Mozambique; this is inference, not survey-backed)
  - import/export of the Commission's Excel communication template, since suppliers commonly exchange that (inferred; not directly evidenced in sources retrieved)

### Gaps
- No survey evidence found on SaaS price willingness, preference for Excel vs web, or offline needs among African producers.
- No customer interview or review data (e.g., G2) for CBAM tools retrieved.
- The official Commission communication template usage rate is unknown.
