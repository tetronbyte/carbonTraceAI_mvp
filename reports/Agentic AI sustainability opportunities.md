# Win adjacent regimes with one plant dataset

The agentic AI products in sustainability that actually ship and get paid for are narrow. Each one is tied to a concrete end state: a permit filing, an interconnection submission, a customs entry, a building set-point or a certification review. The open-ended ESG "copilots" are assistants rather than agents, and almost every accuracy or ROI figure in the space is a vendor claim with no independent check. Money is following the same pattern. Climate venture funding rose to **$26.1bn in H1 2026**, but low-carbon data centres took **34%** of it, the climate-software category grew only **6%**, and carbon-market funding fell **61%** ([CTVC](https://www.ctvc.co/h126-climate-tech-funding-up-55-to-26bn-thanks-to-data-centers/)). Demand is also moving. Entity-level disclosure is shrinking: the CSRD now covers only firms with more than 1,000 employees and more than €450m turnover, and the US SEC climate rule is being rescinded. Product- and shipment-level market-access rules are taking its place: EU and UK CBAM, EUDR, PPWR, the Forced Labour Regulation and battery rules. The economics are unforgiving. The merged SME carbon-accounting leader earns about **€7.5k per customer per year**, exits are going for modest, mostly-stock prices, and businesses that depend on the voluntary carbon market are in distress. Winners sell to intermediaries that already have budgets, not to cash-poor SMEs. I scored thirteen candidate opportunities against a six-part viability checklist. **Only one clears the bar: a multi-regime installation MRV engine.** It runs one plant dataset through EU CBAM, UK CBAM (from 1 January 2027), South Africa's carbon tax (R308/t in 2026) and, later, Türkiye's and India's schemes. It also improves on carbonTraceAI's current CBAM product, because under the carbon tax the liability falls on the producer, who is carbonTraceAI's actual user. Lender-paid data packs are the best experiment for fixing the payer and distribution problem. A supplier "evidence passport" and an energy advisor make sense as modules. EUDR has the highest agent fit and the largest African exposure, but it sells to a different buyer, the market is crowded, and the deadline is eleven weeks away, so the right move is to partner rather than build. Water, adaptation, insurance-risk and TNFD opportunities were not researched, so this ranking cannot speak to them.

The sustainability agents with real traction sit in four clusters, plus a fifth that is just emerging. All of them automate a bounded workflow with a clear owner and a clear cost of delay. Africa appears in this landscape only at the edges. Husk Power says "agentic models optimize generation assets every 30 minutes" across its mini-grids ([Husk Q4 2025](https://www.huskpowersystems.com/quater-reports/q4-report---2025)), but it publishes no fuel, uptime or curtailment results, so this is positioning, not evidence. Digital Green's Farmer.Chat is the most widely deployed LLM advisory tool for African smallholders. The only rigorous test of it, an IFPRI cluster RCT across 600 Kenyan villages, runs until **31 March 2027** ([AEA Registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/17035)). There is no evidence of closed-loop industrial agents in sub-Saharan African factories, where the lack of sub-metering and unreliable grid power make advisory tools the realistic first step.

Hype and substance split cleanly. The products with pull hand a human a finished, checkable artefact: a permit package, an interconnection filing, a customs entry, a set-point. Products whose output is "insight" sell less well. Clarity AI's investor tool, for example, is a research assistant that drafts company briefs ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/clarity-ai-launches-new-sustainability-research-solution-for-investors/)). The Siemens and Schneider industrial copilots generate automation code and help with troubleshooting, and neither publishes energy-savings figures attributable to the LLM ([Hydrocarbon Processing](https://hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2025/05/schneider-electric-unveils-industrial-gen-ai-copilot-in-collaboration-with-microsoft/)). Gartner estimates that only about 130 of thousands of self-described "agentic" vendors are real, and expects more than **40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end-2027** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). Trellis has documented an AI sustainability agent reproducing vague corporate green language, which is a reminder that agents can automate greenwashing as easily as they prevent it ([Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/algorithmic-greenwashing-lessons-from-building-an-ai-agent-for-nature/)).

### Capital is flowing to powering AI, not to AI for sustainability workflows

The headline figures look healthy, but their composition does not favour a sustainability-agent start-up. Sightline/Currence counted **$40.5bn** of climate venture and growth investment in 2025 (+8%). Deal count fell 18% to 1,545, the lowest since 2020, and seed rounds dropped 20% ([Currence](https://www.currence.ai/blog/40-5bn-and-8-uptick-as-power-demand-drives-25-investment)). PitchBook's 2025 figure is $42.2bn, down 1.4% ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.brightspotcdn.com/99/96/badc1db34d4683341eb68a9fc740/q4-2025-climate-tech-vc-trends-preview.pdf)). PitchBook also found that climate-tech software raised **$4B in 2024, against roughly $5.8B a year** in each of the three years before ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q4-2024-pitchbook-analyst-note-climate-tech-software)). Fundraising by climate-specialist VC funds fell nearly **40%** in 2025, and Earthshot reports that 90% of its companies now have generalist co-investors ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/23/climate-tech-vc-fundraising-is-down-almost-40-but-ai-is-driving-deals/)). African climate tech raised **$1.5B in 2025**. About 54% of that was debt, deal count fell for a third straight year to 151, and five energy-access hardware companies have taken about 44% of climate capital since 2019 ([CNBC Africa/Briter](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/media/7784126004558/briter-african-climatet-tech-funding-rose-to-15bn-in-2025)). The closest thing to an African climate-software investor is Catalyst Fund, with **$30M** committed across 28 start-ups ([Technext](https://technext24.com/2026/07/02/catalyst-fund-30m-for-climate-startups/)). Two widely repeated figures, that "28% of climate dollars went to AI-enabled solutions" and that "AI-linked climate tech hit $6.6B", have no traceable primary dataset, and this report does not rely on them.

Exits follow the same logic. Strategic buyers that already own distribution are absorbing the category: Trane bought BrainBox, Carbon Direct bought Pachama ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/carbon-direct-acquires-carbon-project-mrv-platform-pachama/)), Position Green bought Greenomy ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/position-green-acquires-sustainability-reporting-and-compliance-solutions-provider-greenomy/)), and Altana bought Cervo. For a small team in this space, being acquired by an incumbent with channels is a more realistic outcome than independent scale.

## Demand is moving from disclosure reports to market-access rules

The single most important structural change for carbonTraceAI happened in law, not in technology. Omnibus I (Directive (EU) 2026/470) narrowed the CSRD to EU companies with **more than 1,000 employees and more than €450m turnover**, and pushed CSDDD compliance to **26 July 2029** ([Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/business-and-human-rights-insights/2026/02/omnibus-i-the-european-union-concludes-csddd-and-csrd-reforms.html)). Secondary estimates put **80–90%** of previously in-scope companies outside the mandate ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/90-of-companies-no-longer-in-csrd-scope-plan-to-maintain-expand-sustainability-reporting-survey/)). The SEC formally proposed rescinding its climate rule on 29 May 2026 ([EcoVadis summary](https://ecovadis.com/regulations/sec-climate-risk-disclosure-rule/)). A 2026 buyer's guide observes that "every vendor on this page repositioned during 2025 and 2026" towards EUDR, PPWR, CBAM and EcoVadis ([CFO Shortlist](https://www.cfoshortlist.com/reports/esg-csrd-reporting-software)). The rules that survived are tied to a product or a shipment. If you fail them, the goods do not clear the border; you are not merely fined for a late report. One African commentator described the barrier as "a missing dataset at the farm, factory, warehouse or port" ([Africa Sustainability Matters](https://africasustainabilitymatters.com/eus-carbon-border-tax-reforms-set-to-redraw-africa-europe-trade-dynamics-ahead-of-2026-rollout/)).

Three points matter for an Africa-focused team. First, **EUDR** has the deepest African exposure and the most agent-shaped work, but the farm-mapping layer is becoming government-procured. Uganda approved five official traceability providers, including Dimitra ([Dimitra](https://dimitratech.medium.com/uganda-approves-dimitra-as-official-traceability-provider-e2be12ed2f62)). Ethiopia contracted local firm Vulcan ICT for a national coffee platform ([Shega](https://shega.co/news/ethiopia-races-to-meet-imminent-eu-deforestation-regulation-with-coffee-traceability-platform)). The buyer-side tool market is led by osapiens, IntegrityNext and Coolset, with Coolset starting at about €12k a year ([Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/best-6-eudr-compliance-tools-for-2026-supply-chain-due-diligence), a self-interested vendor ranking). The gap that appears to remain is the exporter and cooperative middle layer: linking national plot IDs to ERP batches, mass balance, and DDS packs. That gap is an inference from vendor lists, not a measured market. Second, **UK CBAM** is a near-copy of EU CBAM for the same customers, starting in twelve weeks. Third, **South Africa's carbon tax** is the only regime here where the producer itself pays a cash liability on the same tonnes that CBAM counts. A carbon price paid in the country of production can be deducted from the CBAM obligation ([BLG/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/tax-authorities/1731660/the-worlds-first-carbon-border-adjusted-tax-enters-its-definitive-phase-what-it-means-for-canada)), so the two filings have to agree.

### Operational and market problems are real, but most lack a commercial payer in Africa

Outside compliance, the notes identify five problem areas with genuine scope, and each has a weak point. Industrial energy optimisation has proven economics in OECD markets, but the funded leaders are moving towards hyperscale data centres. African factories mostly lack the sub-metering that closed-loop control needs, so the first value is advisory: anomaly detection, diesel-versus-grid scheduling and leak finding. In African mini-grids the binding constraint is getting committed subsidy actually disbursed, not engineering. The BII–Odyssey facility exists specifically to cover the gap between committed and disbursed DARES subsidies ([BII](https://www.bii.co.uk/en/news-insight/news/bii-and-odyssey-launch-facility-to-accelerate-mini-grid-deployment-in-nigeria/)). Odyssey already hosts the results-based-finance windows, though, so an entrant would be competing with the platform. South Africa's private wheeling market is scaling. Impofu North (112.1 MW) now wheels power to Sasol and Air Liquide ([Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/enel-green-power-rsa-brings-online-330-mw-impofu-wind-farm-cluster-underpinned-by-three-long-term-power-purchase-agreements-ppas-with-sasol-and-air-liquide-a-landmark-renewable-energy-project-for-south-africas-private-sector-2026-07-02/rep_id:5872/company:sawea-2026-04-07)). That creates commercial work in bill reconciliation and Scope 2 evidence, but the opportunity is South Africa-specific.

Carbon markets now have operational Article 6 activity in Africa. Ghana has authorised **12.7 MtCO2e** of ITMOs through 2030 ([Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/546612/)), and Kenya has capped transfers at 10m ITMOs for 2026–2030 ([Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/522403/)). The integrity crisis also makes measured data valuable. A peer-reviewed study found cookstove credits over-credited by about **9.2x**, with metered methodologies closest to reality ([UC Berkeley](https://news.berkeley.edu/2024/01/23/as-carbon-offsets-cookstove-emission-credits-are-greatly-overestimated)), and A-rated credits trade at about $14.80/t against $3.50/t for low-rated ones (Kanop's figures). Climate-finance access is the largest gap by value: Africa receives about **$43.7bn a year, meeting 23% of NDC needs** ([CPI](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/publication/landscape-of-climate-finance-in-africa-2024/)). Yet the only AI proposal tool visible is an NDC Partnership/WRI effort still at the user-needs stage ([NDC Partnership](https://ndcpartnership.org/news/tech-good-leveraging-power-ai-unlock-climate-finance)). An open niche, in other words, but one funded by readiness grants. Smallholder advisory is crowded with donor-funded players. Scrap traceability for CBAM steel is a logical idea, but the notes found no market evidence for it.

## Few of these markets can carry a venture-scale business

The failure and consolidation record tells a consistent story about SME sustainability software: there are too many undifferentiated tools and too few buyers who have to pay. Greenly bought Normative in September 2026. The merged firm serves 4,000+ companies on about **€30M ARR**, roughly **€7.5k per customer**, after the two raised about €115M between them, and Greenly said "the carbon accounting market has grown crowded" ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/greenly-acquires-normative-as-carbon-accounting-consolidation-accelerates/)). Diginex bought Plan A for **€3M in cash plus shares valued at €52M** ([SEC Form 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2010499/000149315226004849/form6-k.htm)). Emitwise "is no longer sold as a standalone product" after its acquisition ([Emitwise](https://emitwise.com/)). Businesses linked to the voluntary carbon market fared worst. BeZero lost **£18M on £4.7M of revenue** ([QCI](https://www.qcintel.com/carbon/article/bezero-reports-loss-of-18m-as-revenue-nearly-doubles-to-4-7m-55015.html)). Sylvera restructured with layoffs ([QCI](https://www.qcintel.com/carbon/article/viridios-to-close-carbon-data-unit-sylvera-confirms-layoffs-32954.html)). Pachama had raised $88M, laid off about 20 staff and was then acquired ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/10/consolidation-begins-to-hit-the-carbon-credit-market/)). Running Tide shut down despite deals with Microsoft, Shopify and Stripe ([Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/deals-microsoft-shopify-and-stripe-fail-keep-carbon-removal-startup-alive/)).

Willingness to pay falls steeply with buyer size and income. Enterprise platforms command real budgets: Watershed's median Vendr contract is about **$70k/yr** ([carbonaccounting.ai](https://carbonaccounting.ai/carbon-accounting-software-cost)), and Persefoni's AWS Marketplace prices run from **$55k to $260k** by revenue band ([knowesg](https://knowesg.com/carbon-accounting-software)). SME tools cluster between about $1,950 and $10,000 a year, and Normative gives its calculator away free to firms with fewer than 50 employees ([Emerald Power](https://www.emeraldpower.io/articles/ultimate-guide-carbon-accounting-software-pricing-in-2026)). The one emerging-market price anchor is an Indian MSME steel exporter that pays a consultant about **Rs 2 lakh (roughly $2.3k) a year** for CBAM ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/how-the-eus-carbon-border-tax-is-reshaping-indian-msmes)). WRI notes that emerging-market climate ventures need more revenue than high-income peers before they attract follow-on capital, and recommends targeting "sustainably profitable SMEs" rather than unicorns ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/technical-perspectives/climate-startup-ecosystem-emerging-markets)). No Africa-specific willingness-to-pay data exists in the notes.

Agentic products also carry thinner margins than classic SaaS. ICONIQ's 2026 survey puts average AI product gross margin at **45% in 2025, rising to a projected 53%** ([1752.vc](https://www.1752.vc/learn/ai-gross-margins-and-inference-costs), secondary). Bessemer's benchmark is 50–60%, against 80–90% for traditional SaaS ([pickaxe](https://pickaxe.co/post/ai-agent-pricing-models), secondary). In compliance work, tokens are not the dominant cost, because filings are infrequent and high-value. Human review and liability are. A per-declaration or per-installation-year price fits outcome pricing well, but only if expert review per output stays small. Intercom's $0.99-per-resolution model shows outcome pricing is now mainstream, and it also shows that the vendor absorbs the cost of failed attempts ([aimdoc](https://aimdoc.ai/blog/intercom-resolution-pricing-explained)).

The vertical-agent winners in regulated domains share one distribution pattern: they sell to an intermediary that already bills the end client. Basis reached a **$1.15B valuation** selling to accounting firms, claiming about 30% of the top 25 US firms ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/02/24/ai-accounting-startup-basis-secures-100m-1-15b-valuation-firms-adopt-agent-based-workflows/)). Gaia Dynamics sells tariff classification to customs brokers and markets a 100% score on the classification section of the broker licence exam ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/gaia-dynamics-hits-major-milestone-with-ai-passing-us-customs-classification-test-with-100-accuracy-302487869.html)). Altana counts US Customs and Border Protection among its customers ([SDxCentral](https://www.sdxcentral.com/press-releases/altana-closes-200m-series-c-to-enable-the-public-and-private-sectors-to-take-command-of-global-value-chains/)). Their ROI is money saved or revenue protected, and their credibility comes from a benchmark or an authority. Legal and accounting valuations do not transfer, however. Sustainability compliance spend is plausibly one to two orders of magnitude smaller than professional-services spend. That comparison is my inference, not a sourced figure, but it implies carbonTraceAI should plan for a capital-efficient, profitable niche business.

The strongest counter-argument deserves a hearing. An osapiens survey found 90% of companies removed from CSRD scope plan to maintain or expand reporting ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/90-of-companies-no-longer-in-csrd-scope-plan-to-maintain-expand-sustainability-reporting-survey/)). Verdantix's public view is that Omnibus "will reduce the urgency – but not the value" of sustainability software ([Verdantix](https://www.verdantix.com/client-portal/report/strategic-focus-unpacking-the-eu-omnibus-and-its-impact-on-sustainability-software)). Both sources sell into the market they describe, and stated intent is not budget. The consolidation prices above are the better evidence.

### Six questions separate a business from a feature

The checklist below distils the failure and success cases into six viability criteria, each scored 0–2. A viable opportunity scores **at least 9 of 12, with no zero on criteria 1 or 2**. I added a seventh, asset-fit score (0–4) to answer the fourth question: does the opportunity reuse what carbonTraceAI has already built? No study links start-up survival to these exact criteria. They are a synthesis of the cases cited above.

| Criterion | Scores 2 when… | Scores 0 when… | Anchor evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1. Mandatory, enforced demand | A dated legal obligation blocks shipments, market access or cash, and has survived rollback | The need is voluntary, or the deadline keeps slipping | CSRD scope cut 80–90%; CBAM and EUDR survived |
| C2. Payer with an existing budget | The payer is a liable producer, importer, bank or intermediary, and the price maps to a quantified avoided cost | A cash-poor SME or a donor is the only payer | ~€7.5k SME ARPU; Basis and Gaia sell to firms |
| C3. Data access and accumulation | The required data is in the ERP, bills or meters, and each customer adds to a proprietary dataset | The data sits with third parties, in the field or on paper, and is unreliable | Missing sub-metering; smallholder plot data |
| C4. Error tolerance and review fit | A human or accredited reviewer catches errors before submission, and review cost keeps gross margin above about 60% | Errors are irreversible and review is expensive | AI margins of 45–60% |
| C5. Distribution | A channel already touches many buyers (brokers, banks, associations, ERP partners), and CAC is under a third of first-year ACV | Direct sales to fragmented SMEs | Intermediary-led winners |
| C6. Defensibility beyond the model | You own the system of record or the submission step, hold accreditation, or have local presence | An incumbent or LLM vendor could ship it as a feature within 12 months | Workiva agents; Planetly, Emitwise and Plan A absorbed |
| Asset fit (0–4) | It reuses the calc engine and ERP/`raw_ref` provenance (0–2) and the same customers and Africa channel (0–2) | It needs a new data stack and a new buyer | carbonTraceAI codebase |

The red flags from the failure cases are: a deadline that has already slipped repeatedly; a value proposition of "visibility" with no money saved; revenue tied to carbon-credit prices; direct sales to SMEs under $5k/yr with no channel; a free competitor for the core calculation; and a plan that needs a climate-specialist mega-round.

### Rank 1: one installation dataset, filed to every carbon regime it touches

This is the only candidate that beats the current product. It does so on the criterion where CBAM is weakest, the payer. Under EU CBAM the importer carries the cash liability, and in 2026 certificates cover only about 2.5% of embedded emissions ([Werksmans/Polity](https://www.polity.org.za/article/south-african-exporters-take-note-2026-08-21)). Under South Africa's carbon tax, the producer pays **R308 per tonne now and R462 by 2030** on the same tonnes ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/corporate/significant-developments)). At today's rate, every 10,000 tonnes misstated is about R3.1m of gross liability before allowances. The carbon price paid domestically is then deductible against CBAM, so a consistent dataset is worth money twice: once on the tax return and once in the EU buyer's price negotiation. South African iron and steel has an estimated **USD 1.66bn** of CBAM-exposed exports ([Lex Africa](https://lexafrica.com/2026/09/south-african-exporters-face-rising-costs-under-eu-carbon-rules/)). UK CBAM starts on 1 January 2027 for the same sectors, without electricity and with indirect emissions deferred to 2029 at the earliest ([RSM UK](https://www.rsmuk.com/insights/tax-voice/are-you-ready-for-uk-cbam-implementation-in-2027)). For carbonTraceAI it is close to a configuration change: a second renderer and a second threshold rule on the same engine. Türkiye's 2026 MRV-only pilot covers exactly the CBAM sectors, and India's CCTS covers 490 entities. Both offer later geographic expansion on the same core, but both have local competitors. The earlier study found Sustrack and Karbonet in Türkiye and CarbonSettle in India, and the Indian price anchor is about $2.3k a year.

The agent role is narrow and valuable. LLMs extract fuel, electricity and process inputs from multilingual invoices and utility bills. They propose mappings between the CBAM installation boundary and production processes and the boundaries and activity categories used for South African tax reporting. They draft a plain-language reconciliation memo explaining why the regimes' totals differ. Deterministic code computes each regime's emissions, the deduction, and every output file. A plant engineer approves the mappings, and a tax practitioner or verifier signs the outputs.

Two risks are material. The EU implementing act on deducting third-country carbon prices was still pending when the earlier study was written ([DEHSt](https://www.dehst.de/EN/Topics/CBAM/CBAM-definitive-regime-2026/cbam-definitive-regime-2026_node.html)). The notes also disagree on whether South Africa's basic tax-free allowance is kept to 2030 or cut from 60% to 50% ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/corporate/significant-developments) vs [SolarXGen](https://solarxgen.co.za/insights/carbon-tax-phase-2-is-now-live-why-the-r308-tonne-rate-and-shrinking-allowances-make-solar-and-bess-the-most-effective-compliance-tool-for-c-i-energy-users-in-2026)). The competitive landscape for South African carbon-tax tooling was not researched. Consultancies such as Anthesis clearly operate there, so the competitor set is probably services firms rather than software. That points to a services-led first sale.

### Ranks 2–5: a channel test, two modules and a partnership

**Lender-paid data packs** score only 8, but they are the most strategically useful experiment, because they attack C2 and C5 together. A bank that finances CBAM-sector exporters, or holds sustainability-linked loans with them, has budget, a portfolio reach that no direct sales team can match, and its own reason to want verified numbers. Industry commentary calls EU Taxonomy alignment "one of the strongest use cases" for generative AI in banks ([IFRE](https://www.ifre.com/esg/2035348/banks-use-ai-for-eu-taxonomy-alignment)), and GFT has built a taxonomy-alignment tool for BPER ([GFT](https://www.gft.com/it/it/about-us/newsroom/press-and-news/2024/press-releases/tassonomia-europea-gft-a-fianco-delle-banche-e-delle-imprese-per-la-valutazione-dei-progetti-en)). However, **no evidence of SLL KPI-monitoring agents or of African lender demand was found**, so this is a hypothesis to test, not a market to enter. The agent drafts KPI evidence narratives and answers lender questionnaires from the store. Code computes KPIs against sustainability performance targets. The borrower's CFO approves before anything is sent.

The **supplier evidence passport** turns the CSRD value-chain cap into a bounded data product. Large reporters cannot ask suppliers with 1,000 or fewer employees for more than the VS standard ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/agreement-on-the-csrd-cs3d-omnibus-package-key-changes-and-implications)), but bank questionnaires, tenders and CDP fall outside the cap ([Fiegenbaum](https://www.fiegenbaum.solutions/en/blog/eu-value-chain-cap-five-gaps-voluntary-standard)). An agent maps each incoming questionnaire to VS datapoints in the store, drafts answers and drafts "beyond the cap" refusal letters, and the supplier approves every outbound answer. It scores zero on defensibility, because EcoVadis, free VSME tools and every reporting suite can do the same. As a standalone product it fails. As a retention feature for #1's customers it costs little.

The **"measure, then reduce" advisor** uses the same meter and ERP data to rank savings by euros of CBAM cost and rand of carbon tax avoided, with IPMVP-style baselines. Fero Labs' Gerdau work shows how alloy and recipe optimisation cuts both cost and embedded emissions ([AI Expert Network](https://aiexpert.network/ai-at-gerdau/)). It belongs later in the roadmap, because the CBAM factor makes the euro savings small until about 2028. It should stay advisory, with no set-point control, until sub-metering exists.

**EUDR** is the hardest call. It scores 2 on mandate and has the largest African exposure, and its work (messy registers, geometry, mass balance, evidence packs) is the most agent-shaped of any regime. It still loses for this team, for three reasons. The buyers are agricultural exporters and cooperatives, not manufacturers. Farm mapping is being procured by governments. And the large-operator deadline is eleven weeks away. The defensible piece is batch-to-plot reconciliation inside an exporter's ERP with `raw_ref`-style provenance. That piece could be licensed to a national-system provider or an EUDR platform rather than built and sold directly.

### What to refuse, and why

Five candidates fail on payer or distribution, not on technology. Methane Regulation MRV sells to a few IOCs and NOCs on a timeline the Commission plans to postpone. Generic CSRD disclosure agents compete directly with Workiva's shipped agents. Mini-grid claims automation would compete with Odyssey, which already hosts the results-based-finance windows. A GCF/GEF proposal agent is a real gap, but readiness grants pay for it, which is grant-and-procurement economics rather than SaaS. Project-side carbon MRV reuses the engine well (fit 3). It ties revenue to the carbon-credit market, though, the segment where BeZero lost 3.8x its revenue and carbon funding fell 61% in H1 2026. It is worth reconsidering only if Article 6 authorisations, not voluntary credits, become the paying use case.

## Vendor claims dominate and four domains remain unresearched

The evidence base comes in three tiers, and the conclusions above lean on the strongest one. **Independent or primary sources** include legal texts and law-firm readings of them (Omnibus I, EUDR, UK CBAM, SA carbon tax), the SEC filing on the Plan A deal, the peer-reviewed cookstove study, CPI's climate-finance data, ICAP's ETS trackers and the registered Farmer.Chat RCT. **Analyst and press sources** cover funding: PitchBook, CTVC/Currence, Briter and Fortune. Their totals disagree with one another, which reflects differences in definition rather than errors. **Vendor claims** cover almost everything about agent performance: BrainBox's 25%, Phaidra's 10–30%, Isometric's "months to hours", Paces' "months to minutes", Facilio's 40%, and osapiens' 80% automation. No independent benchmark of agentic workflows against conventional ML or human workflows was found in any domain, and **no agent product publishes its price**. Several funding rounds appeared only in snippets or aggregators and are excluded here: Makersite's €60M, Climatiq, Second Nature, a $300M Altana round, and Clarity AI's Prosus round.

The gaps that could change the ranking are specific. **Water** (utility non-revenue-water and leak agents), **climate adaptation and disaster response**, **physical-risk agents for insurers and banks** (including African parametric insurance) and **TNFD/nature assessments** were not researched at all. Any of them could hold an opportunity this report cannot see. **LatAm and Southeast Asia** were also skipped, even though they carry heavy EUDR exposure (Brazilian soy and cattle, Indonesian palm and rubber, Vietnamese coffee). For the top-ranked opportunity, the missing facts are the South African carbon-tax tooling landscape, the UK CBAM verification and default-value rules, and the deduction implementing act. For the business case overall, there is no African willingness-to-pay data, no TAM for origin-side compliance tooling, and no quantified hours per compliance task. Several unresolved conflicts also remain open: whether the EU Forced Labour Regulation's risk database is live, Nigeria's ISSB mandate date, Kenya's carbon-registry launch, and Morocco's ISSB status.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "agentic opportunity" should mean for carbonTraceAI. Across every domain, the agents that succeed do not generate insight. They prepare a filing that a liable human signs. That makes the number of filings a single plant dataset can feed the real growth lever, more than the sophistication of the agent. carbonTraceAI's deterministic engine and `raw_ref` provenance are therefore worth more with each regime they serve, because each new regime adds a payer and reuses the same verified tonnes. Run in parallel across regimes, reconciliation becomes the product: explaining why EU CBAM, UK CBAM and South African tax totals differ and proving they agree where they must. And that is exactly the bounded, document-heavy work where an LLM proposal layer earns its keep.

The binding constraint is commercial, not technical. carbonTraceAI's weakest scores, today and in every adjacency, are on payer and distribution, so the most valuable "agentic" move may be a change of buyer rather than a new agent. That buyer is the South African producer who owes carbon tax directly, or the bank that wants verified numbers across a whole loan book. The calendar compresses the decision. UK CBAM starts in twelve weeks, the first EU CBAM declaration falls due in September 2027, and South Africa's carbon tax rises every January to 2030. A team that can show one South African plant reconciled across three regimes before mid-2027 will have proof that no CBAM-only competitor can match.
